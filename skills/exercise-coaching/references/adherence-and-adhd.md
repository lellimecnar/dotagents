# Adherence and habit design (with ADHD-specific adjustments)

## What the evidence says
**Habit formation (Lally 2010):** 82 adults repeated a chosen daily behaviour in a consistent context. Time to 95% of maximum automaticity: median 66 days, range 18–254. Exercise behaviours took about 1.5× longer than eating/drinking ones. Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation; many misses lowered the final automaticity. About half the motivated participants did not perform consistently enough to form a habit. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsp.674 ; summary https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit ; author's caution that "66 days" is an average, not a rule https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally

**Implementation intentions / planning:** if-then plans specifying when, where and how (action planning) plus obstacle plans (coping planning).
- 35 RCTs, general population: SMD 0.35 (small-to-medium); action planning alone did better than action + coping planning in that analysis. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9223740
- Adults RCT meta-analysis: effective when the plan is reinforced during follow-up (SMD 0.25). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6235272
- University students: SMD 0.31, larger in inactive people and with reinforcement. https://mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/16/12457

**Adults with ADHD (qualitative, n=30; Heisz group 2023):** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10156575/
- Barriers: executive dysfunction (forgetting, sustained focus, time management), difficulty *starting*, scheduling never feeling like a good time, intention–action gap even after scheduling, boredom and lack of structure once started, hyperfocus on other tasks, overstimulation before activity, low self-esteem/self-consciousness, appetite changes from medication leaving low energy.
- Facilitators: mood, focus and mental clarity during/after activity; body-doubling and group activity (though some prefer solo to avoid letting others down); stimulating, engaging activity; seeing progress; sense of accomplishment; acting on spontaneous bursts of motivation; preparing in advance (bag packed, food prepped); overt prompts (alarms, check-ins); removing distractions and following a logical sequence; more structure.
- Benefits for adults with ADHD: physical activity improves inhibitory control in adult ADHD (meta-analysis, JOGH 2025 https://jogh.org/2025/jogh-15-04025/). Treat cognitive benefits as a motivator, not a treatment claim.

## Design rules (apply to everyone; mandatory for ADHD)
1. **Decide for them.** One plan, already prioritised. At most one backup. Never a menu to sort. (Rationale: executive-function load and choice overload are documented barriers in adults with ADHD; Heisz.)
2. **Name each session concretely** (what, how long, and when: "20-minute walk after lunch"). Offer one small first step if starting is genuinely unclear or the session has repeatedly stalled; skip routine instructions such as putting on shoes.
3. **Anchor the session to an existing cue** in a stable context: "After [existing event], I'll [do the planned activity] at [place]." (Lally; implementation intentions.)
4. **Floor version**: a 2–5 minute version that still counts on bad days (one song, one set). Protects consistency; one miss is fine, many misses hurt (Lally).
5. **Reduce friction**: equipment out and visible the night before; no setup decisions at start time (Heisz: preparing in advance).
6. **External prompts**: help the person choose a reminder they can set or explicitly authorize, such as an alarm or calendar alert. Offer a check-in or workout partner if they want one (Heisz).
7. **Stimulation and novelty within structure**: music, games, rotating songs/levels, a fixed sequence so no decisions mid-session (Heisz).
8. **Visible progress**: a simple tally (sessions done, reps, minutes) the person can see; celebrate streaks of weeks, not days [coaching practice built on Heisz "seeing progress"].
9. **Ride bursts**: if motivation spikes, allow an extra session but don't raise the baseline plan because of it (Heisz: bursts are real but unpredictable).
10. **Reinforce the plan** at review points (every 1–2 weeks) — reinforcement is where planning effects show (PLOS meta-analysis).
11. **Fix fit before content**: if sessions are missed, move or shrink them before changing exercises.
12. **Expect ~2+ months** before it feels automatic; say so up front so a hard week 3 is not read as failure (Lally).

## Review questions (2–4 weeks)
- Which planned sessions happened, and what prevented the others? Match the fix to the cause: a prompt if forgotten, a better slot if there was no time, a smaller floor if tired, or more variety if bored.
