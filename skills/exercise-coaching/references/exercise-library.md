# Exercise instruction library (core movement patterns)

Format for every exercise: setup → execution cues → common faults → regression → progression → stop signs. Teach the easiest version the person can do with clean form, progress only at the top of the rep range with good form (ACSM 2009 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/; NSCA progression principle https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/8323553f698a466a98220b21d9eb9a65/foundationsoffitnessprogramming_201508.pdf). Poor technique is considered a training-injury risk factor and pattern proficiency is what allows progressive overload (NSCA PTQ 10.2 https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/3d09f06f0b4c4f6fbd8cc382ed1f3d4a/ptq-10.2.1-progressive-strategies-for-teaching-fundamental-resistance-training-movement-patterns.pdf).

Cues marked [coaching practice] are standard practitioner cues not taken from a cited page.

## 1. Squat
**Ladder:** box/chair squat (touch-and-go) → bodyweight squat → goblet squat (dumbbell, jug or backpack held at chest) → slower lowering / pause → split squat.
- **Box squat (NSCA PTQ):** stand slightly in front of a box/bench, feet flat; bend knees, hips and ankles to touch the box, then stand.
- **Goblet squat (ACE https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/362/goblet-squat/):** feet about shoulder-width; weight held vertically in front of chest; elbows close to ribs, back straight; lower until hips are below knees (or to a comfortable depth, ACE https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/5840/exercise-swaps-to-jumpstart-your-workout-routine/); push both feet into the floor to stand. Pick a load for 8–12 reps.
- **Cues:** "sit between your heels", "whole foot on the floor", "chest tall" [coaching practice; heels down and chest up also in ACE youth guide https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/5064/youth-fitness-fitness-training-phase-2/].
- **Faults:** heels lifting, knees collapsing inward, rounding the back at the bottom [coaching practice]. Fix: raise the box, slow down.
- **Stop:** knee or hip pain that is sharp or increases set to set.

## 2. Hinge (hips back, spine long)
**Ladder:** back-to-wall hinge → glute bridge → single-leg bridge → hip hinge with load (backpack/dumbbell Romanian deadlift).
- **Back-to-wall hinge (NSCA PTQ):** stand ~12 in in front of a wall, feet between hip- and shoulder-width, knees unlocked; push hips back to touch the wall, return to standing.
- **Glute bridge (ACE https://acefitness.org/education-and-resources/lifestyle/exercise-library/49/glute-bridge):** on your back, knees bent, feet flat hip-width; gently brace abdominals to flatten low back; exhale and press hips up by squeezing glutes, heels pressing down; avoid pushing hips too high (arches low back); inhale and lower slowly. Progression: single-leg (ACE https://acefitness.org/education-and-resources/lifestyle/exercise-library/145/glute-bridge-single-leg-progression%20).
- **If felt in hamstrings/low back, not glutes:** tilt pelvis first ("belly button to chin"), then peel up butt, low back, mid back; reverse to lower (ACE http://acefitness.org/blog/3494/two-underrated-exercises-you-should-be-doing).
- **Stop:** low-back pain that persists after the set.

## 3. Horizontal push
**Ladder:** wall push-up → incline push-up (counter/table/stairs) → bent-knee push-up → negative (5–6 s lowering) push-up → full push-up. ACE notes high-plank holds (20–30 s) build the trunk foundation, then negatives, then full range. https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/7265/perfecting-the-push-up-for-all-levels/
- **Setup (ACE https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up):** hands shoulder-width, fingers forward or slightly in, shoulders over hands; brace core, squeeze glutes and quads; head in line with spine.
- **Execution:** lower slowly with a rigid torso until chest/chin nears the floor (or the edge of the incline); press up "pushing the floor away" to straight elbows. Elbows close to sides shifts load to triceps and may reduce shoulder stress.
- **Faults:** low back sagging, hips hiking (ACE). Wrist discomfort: grip dumbbell handles instead of flat hands (ACE bent-knee push-up https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/13/bent-knee-push-up/).
- **Progression rule (ACE article):** 2 × 6–8 → build to 10–12 reps → add sets (3–4) → next harder version starting again at 2 × 5–6.
- **Stop:** shoulder pain at the bottom position.

## 4. Horizontal pull
**Ladder:** band or backpack row with chest supported on a bench/bed edge → one-arm row with hand on a chair → two-arm bent-over row → heavier load.
- **Bent-over row (Mayo Clinic https://mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/multimedia/bent-over-row/vid-20084680):** feet about shoulder-width, abdominals tight, knees bent, lean forward at the hips with a straight spine; arms hang under shoulders; raise the weight until the elbow is just below the shoulder; lower slowly. Do not let the shoulder roll forward. ACE barbell version: back flat, pull towards the belly button (https://acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/12/bent-over-row).
- **Faults:** standing up taller as fatigue sets in, jerking the weight, rounding the back [coaching practice].
- **Home load:** backpack with books or a water jug (no purchase needed).
- **Stop:** low-back strain → switch to chest-supported or one-arm-on-chair version.

## 5. Trunk / anti-extension
- **High plank (ACE push-up article):** hands under shoulders, body straight, press the floor away; hold 20–30 s, rest 30 s, 3–4 reps; progress to 4 × 45 s before moving on to harder push-up work.
- **Faults:** sagging hips or piking (same as push-up).

## 6. Carry and single-leg (optional, add after the first 4 weeks)
Suitcase/farmer carry with jugs or bags; split squat or reverse lunge. ACE lists farmer's carry and split squats in its library (https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/equipment/dumbbells/). Cues [coaching practice]: carry — tall posture, shoulders down, walk slowly; split squat — front foot flat, drop the back knee straight down.

## Exercise order and tempo
Large before small, multi-joint before single-joint; moderate tempo (1–2 s up, 1–2 s down) (ACSM 2009). Strength-priority exercises go first in the session (ACSM 2026 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823).
