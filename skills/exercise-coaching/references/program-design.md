# Program design

## 1. Weekly targets (U.S. public-health example, adults 18–64)
Use current guidance for the person's location and population. The first four bullets summarize U.S. guidance; the final bullet summarizes ACSM professional guidance.
- Aerobic: 150–300 min/wk moderate, or 75–150 min/wk vigorous, or an equivalent mix. 1 vigorous minute ≈ 2 moderate minutes. Source: PAG 2nd ed. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30418471 ; CDC https://cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/what-counts.html
- Muscle-strengthening for all major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders, arms) on ≥2 days/wk. Source: CDC https://cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html
- Bouts of any length count; the old 10-minute minimum was removed. Source: https://health.gov/paguidelines/second-edition/10things
- The least active gain the most from even modest increases. Source: PAG (PubMed above).
- ACSM (Garber 2011) adds: flexibility for each major muscle-tendon group ≥2 days/wk (hold 10–30 s, accumulate 60 s per stretch), neuromotor/balance work 2–3 days/wk; moderate ≥30 min × ≥5 d or vigorous ≥20 min × ≥3 d; 500–1000 MET·min/wk. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556 ; flexibility details https://acsm.org/about-acsm/media-room/acsm-in-the-news/2011/08/01/acsm-issues-new-recommendations-on-quantity-and-quality-of-exercise

## 2. FITT-VP
Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type, Volume, Pattern, Progression (ACSM framework, restated in the 2026 resistance stand https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823).

## 3. Intensity tools
| Tool | Light | Moderate | Vigorous | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Talk test | can sing | can talk, not sing | a few words before a breath | CDC https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/ |
| 0–10 effort | <5 | 5–6 | 7–8 | CDC (same) |
| METs | 2–<3 | 3–<6 | ≥6 | CDC; Riebe 2015 |
| RPE 6–20 | 9–11 | 12–13 | ≥14 | Riebe 2015 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26473759/ |
| %HR reserve | 30–<40 | 40–<60 | ≥60 | Riebe 2015 |

Strength effort: reps in reserve (RIR). RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1 RIR, etc. Novices judge RIR less accurately; track it alongside normal data and do not base progression on RIR alone until they are accurate. Main lifts mostly at 2–4 RIR; failure only on the last set of low-risk exercises. Source: Helms 2016 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/

## 4. Resistance training prescription
**ACSM 2026 position stand (overview of 137 reviews):** any resistance training beats none; strength improves with loads ≥80% 1RM, full range of motion, 2–3 sets, exercise early in session, ≥2 sessions/wk; hypertrophy improves with ≥10 sets/wk per muscle and eccentric overload; bands, bodyweight and home programs work. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823 ; summary https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026

**ACSM 2009 (still the practical novice rule set):** novices 8–12 RM, 2–3 d/wk; ~60–70% 1RM for novice–intermediate; increase load 2–10% (smaller for small-muscle exercises) when 1–2 reps over target on two consecutive sessions; large before small, multi-joint before single-joint. Novices can start at 50–60% 1RM or less while learning form. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/

**NSCA principles:** specificity, overload, progression (individual, not a fixed calendar), variation. Progress by load, sets, reps, shorter rest, or harder exercise selection. https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/8323553f698a466a98220b21d9eb9a65/foundationsoffitnessprogramming_201508.pdf

## 5. Minimum effective dose (time-poor)
- Prioritise bilateral multi-joint movements, full ROM, ≥4 weekly sets per muscle group, 6–15 RM loads. Supersets, drop sets and rest-pause roughly halve session time. Warm up with exercise-specific sets only; stretch only if flexibility is a goal. Iversen 2021 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1
- Minimal-dose approaches (below guidelines) still raise strength; "something is better than nothing", "every repetition counts". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11127831
- Beginners: one weekly session with <3 sets can raise strength. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01949-3

**Disagreement:** ACSM 2026 says hypertrophy improves with ≥10 sets/wk; Iversen gives ≥4 sets/wk as the time-efficient floor. I start time-poor beginners at the floor (4–6 sets/muscle/wk) and build towards 10 only if they have time and want size. Reason: adherence first (ACSM 2026's own headline).

## 6. Exercise snacks and lifestyle bursts
- Exercise snacks: isolated ≤1-min vigorous bouts through the day (e.g. stair climbing 60 steps fast, 3×/day, 3 d/wk raised fitness ~5% in 6 weeks in inactive adults). https://journals.lww.com/acsm-essr/fulltext/2022/01000/exercise_snacks__a_novel_strategy_to_improve.5.aspx ; detail https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/966028_3
- 2026 meta-analysis (BJSM): snacks ≤5 min, ≥2 bouts/day, improved cardiorespiratory fitness in inactive people with ~5–67.5 min/wk. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/60/2/133
- VILPA: ~3.4–4.1 min/day of brief vigorous daily-life bursts associated with 22–28% lower mortality (observational, non-exercisers). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-02100-x

## 7. Periodization and deloads
- Deload = "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness" (expert Delphi). Typically every 4–6 weeks for ~7 days by reducing volume (sets/reps), effort (further from failure) or frequency; session duration decreases; coaches say evidence is mostly experiential. Novices/intermediates: more proactive; advanced: more reactive. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399 ; coach interviews https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223/full
- Athlete survey: deloads averaged 6.4 days every 5.6 weeks. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-024-00691-y

**Disagreement / my rule:** the deload evidence comes from strength and physique athletes and is opinion-based. For a beginner at low volume, I do not schedule a fixed deload; I use a *reactive* lighter week (halve sets, keep the slot) when illness, sleep loss, travel or soreness hits, and a planned lighter week every ~6 weeks only once volume is near guideline levels.

## 8. Aerobic and active gaming
| Activity | Measured intensity | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Beat Saber, single trained rater, Expert level | avg 6.24 METs, peak 7.18 (vigorous) | VR Health Institute https://vrhealth.institute/portfolio/beat-saber |
| Beat Saber, healthy adults, lab crossover | ~30% HRR, "very light"; BOXVR boxing reached moderate (~49% HRR) | Virtual Reality 2024 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-024-00981-6 |
| Beat Saber, children 8–12 | light by METs/HRR, moderate by RPE | Godfrey 2025 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39093835 |
| Supernatural (VR fitness) | ~7.9 METs by calorimetry (vigorous) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11185914 |

**Disagreement:** VRHI rates Beat Saber vigorous; controlled lab studies rate it very light to light-moderate. VRHI notes its rater burned more as skill rose and that the rating needs constant movement; its report (as covered by Road to VR https://www.roadtovr.com/playing-beat-saber-burn-number-calories-tennis) suggests turning off the ability to fail songs so each song plays to the end. **My rule:** treat exergames as light until the talk test or 0–10 scale shows otherwise; count only minutes at "can talk, not sing" or harder toward the moderate target. To raise intensity: adjust difficulty, active time or movement range as the game, play space and person's ability allow. For example, a rhythm game may allow longer continuous play, while a fitness game may offer a higher-effort mode.
- VR safety: clear play space; some players have VR sickness that lasts ≥40 min after 50-min sessions; those who get sick after short sessions will after long ones. Wait after play before driving or detailed screen work. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33095182

## 9. Home and no-equipment options
Bodyweight, bands and home programs improve strength and function (ACSM 2026). Load ideas without buying anything: backpack with books, water jugs, tempo (slower lowering), pauses, single-leg versions, more reps, less rest. Present the no-cost option first. If equipment would help, ask about the person's budget and whether they want purchase options; do not assume approval to buy.

## 10. Session template (20–30 min, beginner)
1. Warm-up 3–5 min: easy cardio + 1 light set of the first exercise (Iversen).
2. Superset A: lower body (squat or hinge) + upper push, 1–3 rounds.
3. Superset B: upper pull + trunk, 1–3 rounds.
4. Optional finisher: 5–10 min aerobic or exergame.
Rest 1–2 min between rounds; sets end with ~2–4 RIR.
