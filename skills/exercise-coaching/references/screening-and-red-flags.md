# Screening and red flags

## 1. Emergency: stop and call emergency services
If any of these appear at rest, during or after exercise, stop the session and tell the person to call the local emergency number now. Do not wait to see if it passes.
- Chest discomfort (pressure, squeezing, fullness, pain) lasting more than a few minutes or that goes away and comes back.
- Discomfort in one or both arms, back, neck, jaw or stomach.
- Shortness of breath with or without chest discomfort.
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness, rapid or irregular heartbeat, unusual tiredness. Women more often have breathlessness, nausea/vomiting, back or jaw pain.
Source: AHA https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack

Stroke signs (BE FAST): sudden loss of Balance; Eye/vision change; Face drooping; Arm or leg weakness/numbness (especially one side); Speech slurred or confused; then Time: call the local emergency number immediately and note when symptoms started. Also sudden severe headache, sudden confusion or trouble walking. Do not drive yourself and do not wait to see if it passes.
Source: AHA/ASA https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/stroke/warning-signs-of-a-stroke

## 2. PAR-Q+: official self-screen
Ask the person to complete the current official form at https://eparmedx.com/print-versions-of-par-q. The questions below summarize the 2025 edition for reference; do not use this summary as a questionnaire.
1. Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure?
2. Chest pain at rest, in daily activities, or during physical activity?
3. Loss of balance from dizziness, or loss of consciousness, in the last 12 months? (Answer no if dizziness was from over-breathing, including during vigorous exercise.)
4. Any other diagnosed chronic medical condition?
5. Currently taking prescribed medication for a chronic condition?
6. A bone, joint or soft-tissue problem in the last 12 months that activity could worsen? (No if it no longer limits you.)
7. Has a doctor said you should only do medically supervised activity?

Follow the current official form's instructions. For reference, the 2025 edition gives these decision rules:
- All "no": cleared. Start slowly and build up gradually. Over 45 and not used to vigorous/maximal effort → consult a qualified exercise professional before that intensity.
- Any "yes": complete the follow-up questions indicated on the form. If all follow-ups are "no", start with 20–60 min of low-to-moderate exercise 3–5 days/wk including aerobic and strengthening, building towards ≥150 min/wk moderate. Any follow-up "yes" → seek further information (ePARmed-X+ or clinician) before becoming more active.
- Delay starting: temporary illness (cold, fever); pregnancy (talk to clinician or use ePARmed-X+); any change in health.
- Clearance is valid for a maximum of 12 months (2025 edition; check the interval on the current form) and becomes invalid if the condition changes.
- The PAR-Q+ must not be altered or rebuilt into another survey; point people to the official form.
Sources: https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/PARQPlus2025ImageFile.pdf ; https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/PARQPlus2025Fillable.pdf ; https://eparmedx.com/print-versions-of-par-q

## 3. ACSM preparticipation screening logic (Riebe et al., 2015)
Three factors: current activity, known disease or symptoms, desired intensity. Risk-factor counting is no longer part of screening.

"Exercise participation" = planned, structured activity ≥30 min at moderate intensity on ≥3 days/wk for ≥3 months.

| Current status | No known CV/metabolic/renal disease, no symptoms | Known disease, no symptoms | Any signs/symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inactive | No clearance needed; start light-to-moderate, progress gradually | Medical clearance recommended before starting any intensity | Medical clearance before starting |
| Active | Continue; progress gradually | Continue moderate; clearance before vigorous (if cleared in last 12 months, no repeat needed unless status changes) | Stop exercise; seek clearance before resuming |

Intensity bands used: light 2–<3 METs, RPE 9–11 (6–20 scale); moderate 3–<6 METs, RPE 12–13, noticeable rise in HR and breathing; vigorous ≥6 METs, RPE ≥14, substantial rise.

Signs/symptoms list: pain or discomfort in chest, neck, jaw, arms or elsewhere that may be ischemic; shortness of breath at rest or mild exertion; dizziness or syncope; orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea; ankle edema; palpitations or tachycardia; intermittent claudication; known heart murmur; unusual fatigue or breathlessness with usual activities.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26473759/ ; full text https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/Fulltext/2015/11000/Updating_ACSM_s_Recommendations_for_Exercise.28.aspx

### Where the sources differ
PAR-Q+ flags *any* chronic condition or prescription for follow-up; ACSM 2015 only flags cardiovascular, metabolic (diabetes) and renal disease or symptoms. **I run the PAR-Q+ (self-administered, broader) and use the ACSM table for intensity decisions.** Reason: PAR-Q+ is the stricter, person-completed tool, so I never interpret conditions myself; ACSM gives the clearer intensity rule.

## 4. During-training stop signs (stop the set, rest, reassess)
- Any emergency sign above.
- Sharp or joint pain, pain that worsens through the session, numbness or tingling (practitioner standard; refer to PT/physician if it persists) [general coaching practice].
- Breathlessness, pain, or inability to finish the planned duration = intensity too high; back off and rebuild (Mayo Clinic https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/exercise-intensity/art-20046887).
- Persistent dizziness or nausea after VR play: stop and wait; some players still feel VR sickness 40 min after 50-min sessions (Szpak 2020 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33095182).

## 5. Scope
The trainer coaches healthy-adult exercise. Diagnosis, injury treatment, medication and clinical nutrition belong to licensed professionals. ACSM itself says its screening does not replace clinical judgment (Riebe 2015).
