# Recovery, sleep and exercise

## Sleep need
- Adults should sleep ≥7 h per night regularly; <7 h is associated with weight gain, diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, depression, impaired performance, more errors and accidents. People worried about too little/too much sleep should consult their healthcare provider. AASM/SRS consensus https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434546
- Sleep, nutrition and life stress all change day-to-day strength; autoregulation (RIR) helps absorb that (Helms 2016 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/).

**Coaching rule:** when sleep is short, keep the session but lower the dose (fewer sets, more RIR) rather than skipping — the habit slot is protected; on a run of poor nights, plan a reactive lighter week (see program-design §7).

## Exercise timing and sleep
- Meta-analysis of 23 studies: evening exercise does not harm sleep in healthy adults (slightly more slow-wave sleep), but sleep-onset latency, total sleep time and efficiency may be impaired after **vigorous exercise ending ≤1 h before bedtime**. Stutz 2019 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30374942
- Follow-up meta-analysis on evening high-intensity exercise: no disruption when done 0.5–4 h before bed; ending ~2 h before may help; ending ~1 h before tends to delay sleep onset. https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1087079221001209

**Rule:** moderate evening activity (including exergames at conversational effort) is fine; put the most vigorous activity earlier in the session and finish vigorous work more than 1 h before lights-out, preferably at least 2 h (exactly 1 h is still inside the ≤1 h risk interval); end with 5 min easy.

## Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), CPAP and exercise
- Exercise training reduced apnea severity (AHI −6.3 events/h), improved sleep efficiency, daytime sleepiness and fitness, with minimal weight change (meta-analysis of 5 studies, 129 people). Effect appears independent of weight loss. Iftikhar 2014 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4216726/
- CPAP/APAP is first-line therapy; adherence is its main limitation (same source). Providers should stay in close contact and may recommend behavioural strategies to increase use (AASM patient guide https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Treatment-OSA-with-PAP-Patient-Guide.pdf).
- Expert Delphi: aim for PAP use >6 h per 24 h (shown to improve cognition) and 7–9 h sleep; persistent sleepiness despite use needs clinician review for other causes. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12090005/
- Untreated OSA causes daytime sleepiness and impaired attention while driving; do not drive when tired or sleepy. NHLBI https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea/living-with

**Rules for the trainer:**
1. Exercise is an *adjunct* to sleep-apnea treatment, never a replacement. Never suggest reducing prescribed therapy.
2. If a person reports excessive daytime sleepiness, drowsy driving, or trouble using their device, advise them to contact their sleep clinician directly, and do not schedule demanding sessions that require alertness at those times.
3. Device usage records are private health data. Do not access them by default. Use only information necessary for the request that the person provides or explicitly authorizes you to access, and keep readings out of shared documents.
4. Don't schedule late vigorous sessions that push lights-out later; protecting total sleep time beats squeezing in a workout.

## Soreness and recovery
- Higher-intensity sessions produce more next-day soreness; VR boxing more than Beat Saber, circuit training more than both (Virtual Reality 2024 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-024-00981-6).
- New exercisers: start 1–2 sets with reps in reserve; soreness should fade within a couple of days; persistent or joint pain → stop that movement and refer (screening file) [coaching practice].
- Novices benefit from 2–3 resistance sessions/wk with a day between sessions for the same muscles (ACSM 2009 frequency https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/; the rest-day spacing is coaching practice).

## After VR play
Allow a waiting period after VR before driving or demanding visual tasks; aftereffects usually resolve within 40 min, but ~14% still felt high sickness after 50-min sessions. Szpak 2020 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33095182
