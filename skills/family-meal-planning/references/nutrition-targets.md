# Nutrition targets reference

## Which guidance is current
**Lookup rule:** The U.S. guideline edition, food icon and serving figures below illustrate guidance checked in November 2026. For a live plan, use the applicable issuing authority's current guidance and record its edition and the check date. Verify reference intakes and sports-nutrition figures with their own issuers; they are not all dietary-guideline figures. Apply the worksheet to the verified inputs, not to the illustrative figures.
- The **Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030** were released January 7, 2026 by HHS and USDA, with the message "eat real food" (USDA press release, https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/press-releases/2026/01/07/kennedy-rollins-unveil-historic-reset-us-nutrition-policy-put-real-food-back-center-health ; document: https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf, accessible version https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA_508.pdf).
- **MyPlate was retired** as the federal icon in 2026 and replaced by "The New Pyramid"; MyPlate.gov redirects to RealFood.gov and the MyPlate Plan calculator was not rebuilt (Roth & Serrano, J Nutr Educ Behav 2026, https://jneb.org/article/S1499-4046(26)00466-5/fulltext). USDA's Team Nutrition still uses MyPlate materials for school meals (same source).
- **How I use them:** amounts and limits come from the DGA 2025–2030 only. The DGA 2025–2030 gives food-group serving goals and the New Pyramid; it does not contain a plate-proportion visual, so I do not use or cite a plate split (the old MyPlate half/quarter/quarter picture) as current guidance.
- **Disagreement:** academic nutrition experts welcomed the DGA's limits on highly processed foods, added sugar and refined grains, and noted the food-group servings are largely consistent with earlier editions; they criticised the higher protein emphasis and the full-fat framing (Harvard T.H. Chan, https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/understanding-the-new-dietary-guidelines-for-americans/ ; Stanford Medicine on children, https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2026/05/kids-us-dietary-guidelines-five-things-to-know.html). I follow the official DGA amounts and keep its saturated-fat cap (under 10% of calories), which also limits how much butter and fatty meat I plan; a clinician's individual advice overrides both.

## DGA 2025–2030 daily amounts (2,000-calorie pattern; adjust to each person's needs)
Source: https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf

| Group | Daily goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Protein foods | 1.2–1.6 g protein per kg body weight | at every meal; vary animal (eggs, poultry, seafood, red meat) and plant (beans, peas, lentils, nuts, seeds, soy) sources; bake, broil, roast, stir-fry or grill instead of deep-frying |
| Dairy | 3 servings | full-fat with no added sugars |
| Vegetables | 3 servings | whole forms; frozen, canned or dried with little or no added sugar also count |
| Fruits | 2 servings | limit or dilute 100% juice |
| Whole grains | 2–4 servings | sharply reduce refined grains (white bread, packaged breakfast items, flour tortillas, crackers) |
| Healthy fats | from whole foods; olive oil first, butter or beef tallow listed as options | saturated fat under 10% of calories |

## Limits
- **Added sugars:** no amount is recommended; no single meal over 10 g added sugars; children: no added sugars recommended (DGA). Stanford notes this extends the no-added-sugar recommendation to age 10, from age 2 previously (Stanford, above).
- **Sodium** (DGA): ages 14+ under 2,300 mg/day; ages 1–3 under 1,200 mg; 4–8 under 1,500 mg; 9–13 under 1,800 mg.
- Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages; children avoid caffeinated drinks (DGA).
- Highly processed foods that are salty or sweet: avoid; prioritise home-prepared meals (DGA).

## Children (DGA 2025–2030 special populations)
- Middle childhood (5–10): whole, nutrient-dense foods; full-fat dairy to meet energy needs; no caffeine; no added sugars; make cooking a regular, fun household routine.
- Offer new foods multiple times (DGA says it may take 8 to 10 exposures) and model eating them.
- Infants: about the first 6 months breast milk only, or iron-fortified formula; solids at about 6 months.
- When to switch a child from whole to lower-fat milk is individual; Stanford advises asking the pediatrician (Stanford, above).

## Protein evidence (for reading a trainer's brief)
- DGA 2025–2030: 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf).
- The long-standing RDA is 0.8 g/kg/day (ISSN position stand cites it, below; AP reported the change, https://apnews.com/article/dietary-guidelines-health-agriculture-federal-nutrition-2d8fa56be3c5900fc45116af7c69d786).
- ISSN Position Stand: protein and exercise (J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8): 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day is sufficient for most exercising people; per-meal dose about 0.25 g/kg or 20–40 g of high-quality protein; spread evenly every 3–4 hours.
- **Decision:** the trainer owns the individual's number. I check that the brief's per-meal amount is achievable with family food and say so if not.

## Portion arithmetic
Rough family estimate only, about 7 g protein each (Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, https://eatright.org/health/essential-nutrients/protein/how-much-protein-should-i-eat): 1 oz cooked meat, poultry or fish; ½ cup cooked beans or lentils; 1 egg; 2 Tbsp nut or seed butter; 1 oz nuts or seeds; ¼ cup (2 oz) tofu. A deck-of-cards-sized piece of meat, a small chicken breast or a drained can of tuna is about 3 oz, about 21 g protein. This shortcut under-counts lean poultry and fish, so never use it for target work: size portions from the food's label or a food-composition database (e.g. USDA FoodData Central, https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) for the cooked form actually served, counting cooking fat.

## Worksheet: folding a Nutrition targets brief into the family plan
1. Read the brief: daily energy, protein (g), any per-meal split, foods to favour or limit, deadline or review date. Note who wrote it and when.
2. Check it against hard constraints (allergies, prescribed textures, medical diets). Conflict? Send back; do not plan around it silently.
3. Per-meal protein = brief's split, or daily target / number of meals.
4. For each plate, count protein from sides and add-ons first. Divide the remaining protein target (if positive) by the main item's protein per cooked ounce. For a mixed dish, use total recipe protein divided by measured cooked yield. Recheck the whole plate against the brief, including cooking fats retained in the food when calculating energy.
5. Add the extra protein to the family batch quantity on the grocery list.
6. Breakfast and lunch: suggest two or three repeatable options that reach the per-meal number (eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, leftovers).
7. Report: what changed for that person, what stayed the same for the family, and anything the trainer should revisit.
