# Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) concepts for coaching

EFT is a therapy, delivered by trained clinicians. We borrow its **language for understanding patterns** so suggestions address the need under a fight. We never run EFT stages or deep emotional processing with a family member; that is therapy.

## Evidence base
- Wiebe & Johnson (2016): EFT is a brief, attachment-based couple therapy that "meets or exceeds the guidelines for classification as an evidence-based couple therapy". Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/famp.12229
- Early meta-analysis of four RCTs (Johnson et al., 1999): effect size about 1.3 and 70–73% recovery. Reported in Wiebe & Johnson 2016 (above).
- Spengler et al. (2022), the first comprehensive EFT meta-analysis (20 studies, 332 couples): pre–post d = .93; EFT vs. alternative couple treatments d = .44; pre to follow-up d = .86; about 70% of couples symptom-free at end of treatment; follow-up results heterogeneous, so durability is "tentative". Source: https://www.lbrubacher.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/1-Spengler-Lee-Weibe-Whittenborn-2022-Comprehensive_meta-analysis_of_EFT.pdf
- **Disagreement:** 1.3 (1999, four small RCTs) vs .93 (2022, broader set). I use the 2022 figure because it covers more studies and the authors expected the downward correction.
- Self-help format: the "Hold Me Tight" group program improved relationship satisfaction (d = .81) in mostly non-distressed couples, with some decline at follow-up. Source: Wiebe & Johnson 2016 (above).

## Core idea: fights are protests about connection
- Attachment relationships are ones where "any response is better than none"; when we get no emotional response from a loved one, "we are wired to protest". Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight* (2008); official free excerpt: https://drsuejohnson.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Copy-of-Demon-Dialogue-Free-Content-Giveaway.pdf
- Underneath most fights the question is "Are you there for me?"

## A.R.E.: what partners need from each other (Johnson)
- **Accessible**: Can I reach you?
- **Responsive**: Can I rely on you to respond to me emotionally?
- **Engaged**: Do I know you will value me and stay close?
Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight* (2008); summary at https://jamesmchristensen.com/book-summaries/book-summary-hold-me-tight-by-sue-johnson (secondary; the book is primary).

Coaching translation: a good small act answers one of these with a yes: phone away (accessible), checking back on the thing they were worried about (responsive), choosing them for 20 minutes (engaged).

## The three Demon Dialogues (negative cycles)
| Pattern | Also called | What it looks like | Underlying message |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find the Bad Guy | Mutual attack | Both blame and criticize | "Where are you?" / "I can't face your disapproval" |
| Protest Polka | Demand–withdraw, pursue–withdraw, criticize–defend | One partner protests disconnection (pushes, criticizes); the other defends and pulls away; each move triggers the next | Pursuer: "I can't reach you." Withdrawer: "I can't get it right, so I'll go quiet." |
| Freeze and Flee | Withdraw–withdraw | Both stop trying; numbness and distance | "I'm hurt and afraid; I'll hunker down" |
Sources: https://drsuejohnson.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Copy-of-Demon-Dialogue-Free-Content-Giveaway.pdf ; Conversation 3 excerpt (a couple recognizing their Protest Polka): https://drsuejohnson.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/HoldMeTight_Except_Conversation3.pdf
- Johnson calls the Protest Polka the most common and entrapping pattern, and Freeze and Flee the most dangerous because hope is fading. Source: *Hold Me Tight*, as excerpted above.

## Raw spots
Sensitivities formed when an attachment need was repeatedly ignored; signs are a sudden shift in emotional tone and a reaction that seems out of proportion. Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight*; summary at https://jamesmchristensen.com/book-summaries/book-summary-hold-me-tight-by-sue-johnson

## How we use this in coaching
1. **Make the pattern the enemy, not the person.** If a helper says "she nags, I shut down", reflect the cycle without blame: both moves make sense; each triggers the other.
2. **For the withdrawer:** the smallest cycle-breaker is to stay present and say one true sentence ("I go quiet because I hate letting you down, not because I don't care"), or to come back and repair after a break of at least 20 minutes, not to disappear.
3. **For the pursuer (when coaching that person):** a soft start-up naming the need under the complaint ("I miss you") instead of the criticism.
4. **Freeze and Flee** (both withdrawn, little hope) → referral signal; suggest a licensed couples therapist (EFT- or Gottman-trained) alongside one small kindness.
5. Never ask a family member to do deep "revisiting a rocky moment" work through an agent. That belongs with a therapist.
