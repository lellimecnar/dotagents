# Special strain: children with significant needs; ADHD in one partner

These apply to many households. Use them to set realistic expectations and to size suggestions. Never use them to label a specific couple.

## A. Parenting a child (or children) with significant needs

### Divorce risk: sources disagree
| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Parents of children with autism: divorce 23.5% vs 13.8% in a matched comparison; risk stayed high through the child's adolescence and early adulthood, while it fell after about age 8 in comparison families | Hartley et al. 2010, https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019847 |
| Large US sample (~78,000 parents, 913 with an autistic child): separation as common as in other families; symptom severity made no difference; the "80% divorce rate" is a myth | Kennedy Krieger Institute, https://www.kennedykrieger.org/stories/interactive-autism-network-ian/whats-truth-about-autism-and-marriage |
| Meta-analysis (Risdal & Singer 2004): higher divorce/discord among parents of children with disabilities, but a small effect (d = .21) | As cited in Hartley et al. 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26505872/ |
| 50-year population study: divorce not elevated on average for parents of children with developmental disabilities (higher in small families) | Hartley et al. 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26505872/ |
| Review: evidence of higher divorce is "weak and inconsistent"; most such marriages are normal; the same things predict healthy marriages as in the general population | Sobsey 2004, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ848190.pdf |

**What I follow:** the risk is at most modestly elevated and not destiny. I never tell a couple their child endangers their marriage and never frame a child as the problem.

### Where the strain actually shows up (most useful for coaching)
- Daily-diary study (174 couples with an autistic child vs 179 comparison couples): parents of autistic children spent **about 21 fewer minutes a day** together (~128 hours a year), felt less close, and had **fewer positive** interactions (jokes, meaningful talk, intimacy) but **not more negative** ones, and felt as supported. Positive-to-negative ratio about 3:1 vs 4.4:1. Sources: https://news.wisc.edu/researchers-gain-insight-into-day-to-day-lives-of-parents-raising-children-with-autism ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5433904
- On days with more partner support and positive couple interactions than usual, both parents reported lower negative affect; a day with more time together was linked to better mood for mothers of autistic children but not comparison mothers. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5433904
- Spillover (176 couples): days with more negative couple interactions came with more parenting stress for both parents; fewer positive interactions came with more parenting stress for mothers. Recommended target: positive couple interactions on stressful parenting days. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5122470
- Hartley lab's practical implication: help parents carve out time to share feelings and thoughts, not only to work through daily demands. Source: https://hartleylab.waisman.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/816/2017/12/2017HartleyLabNewsletter.pdf
- Parents of children with ADHD: twice as likely to divorce by the child's age 8 (Wymbs et al. 2008); in an experiment, managing a child acting out ADHD-like behavior **caused** more negative parent-to-parent communication. Source (review): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33421168

### Coaching rules that follow
1. **Aim for more small positives, not just fewer fights.** The measurable deficit is positive moments and time together: jokes, a hug, a text, ten minutes of real talk.
2. **Protect micro-time.** If a full date is impossible, an at-home date after bedtime or 20 minutes during a supervised window counts.
3. **Name the childcare problem in every date plan**: who supervises each child, what familiarity or training a sitter needs, whether the window depends on a therapist, respite provider or relative. A generic babysitter is often not a real answer.
4. **On hard caregiving days, choose kindness that reduces load or is effortless to receive**, not something that asks the tired partner for energy.
5. **Talk about something other than the kids sometimes.** Logistics talk is not connection.
6. Mention local respite and caregiver programs when relevant. Offer to verify local access routes when requested.

## B. ADHD in one partner

### Evidence
- Wymbs et al. (2021) review, *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy*: adults with ADHD report lower marital satisfaction and adjustment, more conflict and more breakups; ADHD is also associated with higher intimate-partner-violence perpetration and victimization (see `safety-and-referral.md`). In Eakin et al. (2004), 96% of spouses said symptoms interfered in at least one area (household organization/time, child rearing, communication) and 92% reported compensating. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33421168
- Same review: when self-control was experimentally depleted, adults with ADHD communicated less positively and more negatively with partners. Fatigue and the end of the day are high-risk windows. Source: same.
- Only one small, uncontrolled trial has tested ADHD treatment integrated into couples therapy; results were encouraging. Source: same.
- Women with ADHD report more relationship difficulty and higher divorce than men with ADHD; male non-ADHD partners rated ADHD behaviors as more impactful. Source: https://psychiatry.duke.edu/duke-center-girls-women-adhd/research/research-spotlights/study-summarizes-research-adhds-impact

### Parent-child dynamics (practitioner model)
- Melissa Orlov (CHADD): over time the non-ADHD partner takes on more responsibility ("easier to do it myself"), resentment grows, and the ADHD partner's status drops from "cherished, competent adult partner" to "resented, unreliable, and hard to trust". One partner oversees and reminds; the other follows or rebels. Source: https://chadd.org/attention-article/becoming-partners-again
- First rule of her "Power Sharing Navigation": **the person who is doing the action is in charge** (no overseeing). Source: same.
- Signs and remedies for each role: https://adhdmarriage.com/blog/what-parent-child-dynamics-and-it-your-relationship

### Coaching rules (when the helper is the ADHD partner)
1. **One act, tied to an existing anchor** (a time they're already home, a routine they already do), the act named concretely. Use the person’s preferred reminder method rather than asking their partner to manage reminders.
2. **Prefer acts that take a whole task off the partner's mental load, owned end to end**: start, finish, close the loop without being reminded. Reliability is itself the kindness; it rebuilds trust.
3. **Visible, specific appreciation for the partner's compensating work** (they often carry an invisible load).
4. **Avoid hard conversations when depleted** (late evening); choose a calmer time or take a break of at least 20 minutes.
5. **Never diagnose** either partner. Treatment questions go to the person's clinician.

### When the helper is the non-ADHD partner
Suggest stepping out of the reminder role on one task, appreciating effort, and using a gentle start-up. Refer to an ADHD-informed couples therapist or clinician if the pattern is entrenched.
