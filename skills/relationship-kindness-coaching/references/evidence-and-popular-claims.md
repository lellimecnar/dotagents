# Evidence strength: what to trust, what to soften, what to drop

## Strong enough to lean on
### Couple therapy works
- Roddy et al. (2020) meta-analysis, 58 studies / 2,092 couples: couple therapy has a large effect on relationship satisfaction (pre–post Hedges g = 1.12); waitlist couples do not significantly improve (g = 0.12); gains generally maintained; greater baseline distress → larger gains. Limitation: only opposite-sex couples. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32551734
- Non-randomized "real-world" studies show smaller (medium) effects, g ≈ 0.52. Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/famp.12889
- Implication: when a couple is stuck, waiting does not help; refer.

### Responding to good news (capitalization)
- Gable, Reis, Impett & Asher (2004): sharing positive events raises well-being; when the partner responds **active-constructively** (enthusiastic, asks questions) relationship quality is higher; passive-constructive ("says little, but I know he/she is happy for me") was *negatively* associated with relationship quality, as were destructive responses. Source: https://doi.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.87.2.228 (PDF: https://sas.rochester.edu/psy/people/faculty/reis_harry/assets/pdf/GableReisImpettAsher_2004.pdf)
- Same paper cites Gable et al. (2003): positive acts the partner does **not notice** had no effect on their mood. Make kindness visible.
- Gable, Gonzaga & Strachman (2006): responses to a partner's good news were more closely related to relationship well-being and to breakups two months later than responses to bad news. Source (University of Rochester Medical Center summary): https://urmc.rochester.edu/behavioral-health-partners/bhp-blog/february-2024/good-news-sharing-and-responding
- How-to (Greater Good in Action): eye contact, positive emotion, enthusiastic comments, open-ended questions, note positive implications. Source: https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/practice_as_pdf/capitalizing_on_positive_events?printPractice=Y

### Gratitude
- Algoe, Gable & Maisel (2010), *Personal Relationships*: in couples' daily diaries, a partner's thoughtful acts → gratitude → next-day increases in relationship satisfaction and connection (a "booster shot"); indebtedness did not help. Source (news summary; journal DOI 10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01273.x): https://psychcentral.com/news/2010/05/25/daily-appreciation-helps-romance/14044.html
- Algoe et al. (2016): across 370 recorded conversations, **other-praising** (crediting the partner's qualities: "you're so thoughtful") was the active ingredient in expressed gratitude; partners felt more understood, cared for and loved. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4988174

## Real but over-claimed: soften when you use it
### Gottman's "90% divorce prediction"
- Gottman Institute: predictions replicated across seven longitudinal studies with over 90% average accuracy. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-empirical-basis-for-gottman-method-couples-therapy/
- Heyman & Slep (2001): such accuracy came from equations fitted on the same small samples; on cross-validation, accuracy and predictive value "drop precipitously". Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1622921
- Heyman (2001): in a large national data set, a 90%-accurate equation lost about a third of its accuracy on cross-validation and was right only 29% of the time it predicted divorce. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1435728/
- **What I follow:** the behaviors (contempt, criticism, defensiveness, stonewalling, failed bids) are well-studied **risk markers** and good coaching targets. I never quote prediction percentages to a family member and never forecast a marriage.

### The 5:1 ratio
Measured during conflict discussions; widely repeated as an everyday quota. See `gottman-method.md`. Use it as "negatives weigh more; add positives", not as arithmetic.

### Bids 86% vs 33%
From one Gottman newlywed sample (institute blog). The peer-reviewed bids paper (Driver & Gottman 2004) is correlational, 49 couples. Use as illustration, not law. Sources: https://www.gottman.com/blog/turn-toward-instead-of-away/ ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2004.00024.x

### "Six hours a week"
From follow-ups of workshop couples (descriptive, not a controlled trial). Good structure for rituals; promise no outcomes. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/6-hours-a-week-to-a-better-relationship/

## Popular but weakly supported: use only as a menu
### The 5 Love Languages (Chapman)
- Impett, Park & Muise (2024), *Current Directions in Psychological Science*: the evidence does **not** strongly support the three core claims (each person has one primary language; there are exactly five; couples are happier when partners match). Expressions of all five were associated with satisfaction regardless of preference, with very little evidence of matching effects (Chopik et al., 2023). Proposed replacement metaphor: love as a **balanced diet** that needs all the nutrients. Sources: https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2024-70884-002 ; https://emilyimpett.com/s/2024-Current-Directions-Impett-et-al.pdf ; https://utoronto.ca/news/little-evidence-linking-five-love-languages-healthy-relationships-researchers-say
- Chapman's original sample was white, religious, traditional couples; it misses, for example, supporting a partner's personal goals. Source: University of Toronto article (above).
- **What I follow:** the five categories (words, time, service, touch, gifts) are a handy idea menu. I vary the type of kindness, use what the partner has actually said they value, and never claim that matching a quiz result is the key.

## Practitioner sources (useful, lower tier)
- Melissa Orlov / CHADD on ADHD "parent-child dynamics" (clinical observation, not trials). See `special-strain.md`.
- Gottman Institute blog posts are written by a commercial practitioner organization: primary for *what Gottman teaches*; look to peer-reviewed work for *how strong the evidence is*.
