# Gottman Method: the research-backed concepts used for coaching

Source priority: The Gottman Institute (primary for *what Gottman teaches*, but a commercial practitioner organization; see `evidence-and-popular-claims.md` for the independent critique), then peer-reviewed papers.

## Sound Relationship House (the frame)
Seven levels: Build Love Maps; Share Fondness and Admiration; Turn Towards Instead of Away; The Positive Perspective; Manage Conflict; Make Life Dreams Come True; Create Shared Meaning. The two walls are trust and commitment.
Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-sound-relationship-house-build-love-maps/

Coaching use: the lower floors (friendship) are where small daily kindness lives; most suggestions target floors 1–3.

## Bids for connection and turning toward
- A bid is "any attempt from one partner to another for attention, affirmation, affection, or any other positive connection"; bids range from a smile to a request for help, and often carry subtext. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/turn-toward-instead-of-away/
- In Gottman's newlywed study, couples still married at six years had turned toward each other's bids 86% of the time; couples who divorced averaged 33%. Source: same.
- Peer-reviewed basis: Driver & Gottman (2004) coded bids and turning toward / away / against in 49 newlywed couples; playfulness and enthusiastic responses in everyday moments were linked to humor and affection during later conflict. The authors state the data are correlational and causation is not shown. Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2004.00024.x
- Responses: turning toward (engage), turning away (ignore), turning against (irritable). Same source.

Coaching rule: the cheapest high-value act is answering the next small bid fully: eye contact, a question, phone down.

## The Four Horsemen and their antidotes
| Horseman | What it is | Antidote | Example of the antidote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Criticism | Attack on character, not a specific behavior ("you never… you're selfish") | **Gentle start-up**: "I feel… I need…", a complaint without blame | "I'm feeling left out tonight and I need to vent. Can we talk about my day?" |
| Contempt | Speaking from moral superiority: sarcasm, mockery, eye-rolling, name-calling | **Build a culture of appreciation and respect**; "small things often" | "I know you've been busy, but could you…? I'd appreciate it." |
| Defensiveness | Righteous indignation or innocent victimhood to ward off attack; really a way of blaming | **Take responsibility**, even for part of it | "You're right, I can be more flexible." |
| Stonewalling | Withdrawing, shutting down, usually when flooded | **Physiological self-soothing**: call a break, at least 20 minutes, do something calming, do not rehearse the fight | "I'm overwhelmed and need a break. Give me twenty minutes and we'll talk." |

Sources: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-recognizing-criticism-contempt-defensiveness-and-stonewalling/ and https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-the-antidotes/
- Gottman calls contempt "the single greatest predictor of divorce". Source: first link above.

## Flooding and breaks
- Flooding: heart rate at or above about 100 bpm during conflict makes it hard to process what the partner says. Sources: https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-part-4 and https://www.gottman.com/blog/weekend-homework-assignment-physiological-self-soothing
- Julie Gottman: for very athletic people, about 85 bpm. Say "I'm flooded" (never "you're flooded"), name when you'll come back, take 20–30 minutes, at most 24 hours. Source: https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/how_to_fight_without_hurting_your_relationship
- Break length: at least 20 minutes, not more than 24 hours (past that it turns into avoidance). Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-part-4
- Lab evidence: couples interrupted and asked to read for half an hour came back with lower heart rates and more positive interaction. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-the-antidotes/

## Repair attempts
- A repair attempt is "any statement or action — silly or otherwise — that prevents negativity from escalating out of control"; masters repair early and often. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/r-is-for-repair/
- Ability to rebound or repair after conflict became a marker of a couple's emotion regulation in the original lab studies. Source: https://www.gottman.com/about/research/couples/

Coaching rule: repair fits the couple's own style (humor, a touch, "can we start over?"). Suggest one, sized to the moment, after both are calm.

## Love maps
- Knowing the details of a partner's world (stresses, friends, hopes, fears, what they did today) and keeping it updated. Couples with detailed love maps are better prepared for stress and conflict. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-sound-relationship-house-build-love-maps/
- Example prompts from the source: "What stresses your partner right now?", "What is your partner's fondest unrealized dream?", "Describe in detail what your partner did today or yesterday."

## Fondness, admiration and the positive perspective
- "Small Things Often": regular appreciation, gratitude, affection and respect build a positive perspective that buffers negative moments. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-the-antidotes/
- Admiration journal: note something small and connect it to a trait you admire. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/6-hours-a-week-to-a-better-relationship/

## The 5:1 ratio (read carefully)
- Gottman: "for every negative interaction **during conflict**, a stable and happy marriage has five (or more) positive interactions"; a ratio of 1:1 or less during conflict signals trouble. Positive interactions listed: be interested, express affection, demonstrate they matter, intentional appreciation, find agreement, empathize and apologize. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-magic-relationship-ratio-according-science/
- The ratio is often repeated as an everyday quota; Hartley et al. (2017) applied a 5:1 benchmark to daily interactions (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5433904). **I follow Gottman's own wording**: a reminder that negativity weighs more than positivity, never a score to compute.

## Perpetual vs. solvable problems
- 69% of relationship conflict is about perpetual problems rooted in personality or lifestyle differences; the goal is dialogue with acceptance and humor, not resolution; mishandled perpetual problems become gridlocked. Sources: https://www.gottman.com/blog/managing-conflict-solvable-vs-perpetual-problems and https://www.gottman.com/blog/debunking-12-myths-about-relationships/
- The Gottmans: of perpetual fights, 16% become gridlocked. Source: https://www.psychotherapynetworker.org/article/the-gottmans-on-how-to-fight-right
- Gridlock is a referral signal (see `safety-and-referral.md`).

## Rituals of connection: "six hours a week"
From follow-ups of workshop couples whose relationships kept improving (Gottman Institute; descriptive, not an RCT):
| Ritual | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Partings | Learn one thing happening in the partner's day before saying goodbye | 2 min × 5 workdays |
| Reunions | A hug and kiss of at least six seconds, then a stress-reducing conversation of at least 20 minutes | 20 min × 5 workdays |
| Appreciation and admiration | Daily genuine appreciation (admiration journal) | daily |
| Affection | Kiss, hold, touch | daily |
| Weekly date | Relaxed, low-pressure time to reconnect | weekly |
| State of the union | Weekly check-in on what went right and what needs attention | weekly |
Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/6-hours-a-week-to-a-better-relationship/ (partings, reunions and appreciation read directly; affection, date and state-of-the-union rows from the same article as summarized in search results; exact minutes for those rows not verified, see Gaps).

## Stress-reducing conversation (the "how was your day" talk)
- 20–30 minutes; talk about stress **outside** the relationship; don't discuss the marriage; take turns; understanding before advice; side with your partner ("don't side with the enemy"). Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-one-daily-talk-that-will-benefit-your-marriage/
- Original lab finding: a woman's harsh start-up in the conflict talk was predictable from her husband's disinterest or irritability in the preceding events-of-the-day talk. Source: https://www.gottman.com/about/research/couples/

## Help-seeking
- Gottman reports that fewer than 10% of divorcing couples ever talk to a professional, and that couples therapy is now effective. Source: https://www.gottman.com/blog/debunking-12-myths-about-relationships/
