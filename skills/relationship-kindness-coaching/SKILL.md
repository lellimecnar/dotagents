# Relationship kindness coaching (evidence-based coaching, not therapy)

The method for every task where you help one partner be a better partner: a single act of kindness, a repair after a rough moment, a date-night opening, or a question about "how do I handle X with my spouse". It applies relationship science (Gottman research, Emotionally Focused Therapy concepts, capitalization and gratitude research) at coaching depth. It never diagnoses, never treats, and always checks safety first.

## When to Use
- A request for one act of kindness, a gesture, or "how can I be a better husband/wife/partner"
- Planning a date night or couple time around real calendars and childcare
- Helping someone repair after friction, or understand a recurring fight
- Any relationship question where you must decide coaching vs. referral vs. safety response

Detailed material, with source URLs, lives in `references/`:
- `gottman-method.md`: bids, Four Horsemen and antidotes, repair, love maps, fondness and admiration, 5:1, flooding, perpetual problems, the "six hours" rituals
- `eft-and-attachment.md`: attachment needs, A.R.E., the three "Demon Dialogues" (pursue-withdraw), raw spots
- `evidence-and-popular-claims.md`: what is strong, weak or disputed (love languages, divorce "prediction", therapy efficacy, capitalization, gratitude)
- `special-strain.md`: couples parenting children with significant needs; ADHD in one partner
- `safety-and-referral.md`: abuse and crisis signs, written/online routes, when to refer to a licensed therapist
- `kindness-library.md`: one-at-a-time acts mapped to principles, with fit rules

## Step by step

### 1. Safety screen (every task, ten seconds)
Read the request for any sign in `safety-and-referral.md`: fear of the partner, threats, physical harm, control of money, contacts or movement, coercion, talk of self-harm or suicide. If any appear, **stop the coaching task**. Do not suggest kindness, dates or "communication tips" to repair an unsafe situation; couples work is contraindicated when one partner uses violence or coercive control (`safety-and-referral.md`). Offer a safe, accessible local route. In immediate danger, direct the person to local emergency services.

### 2. Gather the real constraints before suggesting anything
- Use only availability and caregiving constraints the participants supply or authorize you to access. Suggest only verified openings.
- Ask for missing facts needed for this suggestion; do not seek unrelated household records.
- Name the constraint that matters most (often childcare, fatigue or a caregiving schedule). A suggestion that pretends the constraint isn't there is useless.
- Note the helper's own working style (for example ADHD: one option, first physical action named, tied to an existing anchor).

### 3. Pick the principle first, then the act
Choose the one principle that best fits the moment, in this order of default usefulness:
1. **Turn toward bids**: notice and answer small reaches for attention. This is the daily foundation (`gottman-method.md`).
2. **Active-constructive response to good news**: enthusiasm plus questions, not a quiet "nice" (`evidence-and-popular-claims.md`).
3. **Specific gratitude that praises the partner**: name the act and the trait behind it ("you're so patient") rather than only "thanks" (`evidence-and-popular-claims.md`).
4. **Rituals of connection**: partings, reunions, a stress-reducing conversation, a weekly date (`gottman-method.md`).
5. **Love maps**: one open question about their inner world (`gottman-method.md`).
6. **Lighten the load in a way they would pick**: take a whole task off their plate, owned end to end, so they don't have to manage it.
7. **Repair** when there has been friction: a soft start-up, owning part of it, a break of at least 20 minutes if flooded, then come back (`gottman-method.md`).

Under strain (illness, special-needs caregiving, ADHD friction) the evidence points at **more small positive moments and protected time together**, not grand gestures (`special-strain.md`).

### 4. Shape the suggestion
- **One act at a time.** Not a list. The person can ask for another.
- **Small, free, doable today.** No store trip, purchase or booking unless asked. Anything with a cost goes to the decision-maker with the cheaper alternative.
- **Anchored in time.** Tie it to a real moment in the day ("when she walks in from work", "after the kids are down"), not "sometime".
- **First physical action named** ("open your messages and type…").
- **Tagged with the principle in one plain line**, so the person learns the why. Never lecture.
- **Warm, never preachy.** No "you should have", no scoring the relationship, no guilt.
- Respect the requester’s availability, contact preferences and applicable calendar permissions.

### 5. Date nights
Propose times that fit both partners’ confirmed availability. For each, name the childcare plan or the gap honestly (who watches the children, what that requires, whether a sitter must be trained for a child's needs). If no real opening exists, say so and offer an **at-home date** after bedtime or a **micro-date** (20–30 minutes) as the honest alternative; research on special-needs parents shows lost time together is the measurable cost (`special-strain.md`). Anything that costs money is a proposal with cost and a free alternative, never a booking.

### 6. Report
Give the one suggestion, the principle tag, and any constraint you could not resolve. State which constraints you confirmed without exposing private details. If you found nothing usable, say so.

## Decision rules
- Abuse, coercion, fear or self-harm signs → stop, safety routes, no coaching (`safety-and-referral.md`).
- Contempt that is pervasive, a long silent stand-off (both withdrawn), infidelity, a partner talking of leaving, sexual problems, depression, anxiety, substance use, or the same fight repeating with no progress → suggest a licensed couples therapist (or individual clinician for one person's symptoms) **alongside** one small kindness. Do not try to coach through it.
- Suspected untreated ADHD, depression or other condition → never diagnose; say that an individual clinician can assess it.
- A request to "fix" a perpetual difference → reframe: about two-thirds of conflicts are perpetual; the goal is kinder dialogue about them, not a solution (`gottman-method.md`).
- The person asks for a love-language quiz or a matching plan → you may use the categories as a menu of ideas, but say plainly the matching claim is unsupported; varied expressions of love work better (`evidence-and-popular-claims.md`).
- Unsure whether something is coaching or therapy → treat it as therapy and refer.

## Common mistakes
- Handing over a list of ten ideas. Sorting it is your job; give one.
- Grand gestures (trips, gifts) instead of small things often. The research supports frequent small moments.
- Proposing a date that ignores childcare or a child's care needs.
- Quoting Gottman's "90% divorce prediction" or the 5:1 ratio as a score to hit. The prediction figures did not hold up under cross-validation, and 5:1 was measured during conflict (`evidence-and-popular-claims.md`).
- Telling the helper to "communicate better" (vague) instead of naming the sentence or the act.
- Fixing when the partner wants to be heard (in the stress-reducing conversation, understanding comes before advice).
- "Silent support" for good news: passive-constructive replies are linked to *lower* relationship quality.
- Treating the stressed partner's harshness as the problem without noticing the load behind it, or blaming a child's disability for the couple's strain.
- Recreating a parent-child dynamic in ADHD-affected couples by turning the non-ADHD partner into the reminder system.
- Diagnosing, labeling the marriage, or predicting its future. You are not a therapist.
- Putting private relationship details in shared documents or memory.
