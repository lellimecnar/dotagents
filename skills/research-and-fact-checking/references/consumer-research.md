# Consumer research: product and service comparisons

## What the evidence says about reviews
- Average star ratings are weak quality signals. Across 1,272 products in 120 categories, average Amazon
  ratings correlated poorly with Consumer Reports lab scores; for two random products there was only a
  57% chance the higher-rated one scored higher at CR; differences under 0.40 stars were unrelated to CR
  scores (50%); even differences over one star matched only about 65% of the time. Ratings were often
  based on too few reviews, did not predict resale value, and were higher for pricier and premium-brand
  products after controlling for quality. Price was more strongly related to CR scores than the
  average rating. [1]
- Consumers overweight the average rating and ignore sample size ("illusion of validity"). [1]
- Fake and manipulated reviews are common enough that the FTC banned them by rule (16 CFR Part 465,
  U.S. federal rule effective 2024-10-21; verify the current eCFR text before applying it): covered conduct includes fake or false reviews (including AI-generated fakes), incentives expressly or implicitly conditioned on a particular sentiment,
  undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, suppression of negative
  reviews, and fake social-media influence indicators. [2] A ban is not elimination: treat it as a list
  of tactics to look for.

## Source ladder for products
| Rank | Source | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manufacturer spec sheet, manual, warranty text; regulatory labels and listings (EnergyGuide, ENERGY STAR product finder, DOE/FTC rules, recall databases) | Hard facts: capacity, efficiency rating, install requirements, warranty terms, legal changes |
| 2 | Independent lab testing that buys products itself and takes no ads (Consumer Reports' stated model: "We buy all the products we rate"; no paid advertising; no free samples — 2012 statement, re-check current policy) [3] | Performance and reliability comparisons |
| 3 | Professional reviewers with disclosed methods (check affiliate income and free-sample policy) | Hands-on usability, shortlists |
| 4 | Owner reviews and forums | Failure patterns, service experience, install gotchas; colour only |

## How to read owner reviews
1. Don't lean on the average; read the lower-rated and most recent reviews for repeated failure
   modes (same part, same month of ownership). This is practical advice, not a threshold from [1];
   no star or date cutoff is established, so pick what the review mix supports.
2. Check sample size and spread together: how informative an average is depends jointly on the
   number of ratings and their variance. Few ratings with high variance match lab scores less well.
   [1] sets no universal review-count cutoff, so don't state one.
3. Look for FTC-banned patterns: reviews that read alike, "received free for honest review" without
   balance, review hijacking (reviews for a different product variant), sudden bursts. [2]
4. Check whether the seller responds and whether complaints are about the product or the retailer.
5. Separate product failures from install errors and from shipping damage.

Rules:
- Always say what an advertised "savings" figure is compared against. Agencies often compare the efficient
  option with the least efficient baseline, which may not be what the household has now.
- Re-check every incentive, rebate and tax credit on the official page the day you write. Agency fact
  sheets and marketing pages can keep advertising a credit after the law ends it; confirm the effective date in the current governing rule.
- Use the household's own rates when known; otherwise state the national assumption the source used.
- Name the cheaper alternative and the do-nothing option.
- Prepare the cart or quote and hand the decision to the authorized purchaser; do not buy.

## Decision rules
- Safety-critical products (car seats, smoke/CO alarms, helmets): rely on standards certification and
  independent tests, never on star ratings. [1] shows star ratings and lab results diverging for such
  categories.
- Services (internet, insurance, contractors): primary sources are the contract terms, published price
  sheets, licence lookups and regulator complaint data; reviews are for service patterns.
- If a choice hinges on install conditions (space, venting, wiring), say so first; the best product that
  cannot be installed is not an option.
