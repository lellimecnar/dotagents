# Writing the cited report with calibrated confidence

## Layout (use the requester's layout if they define one)
Answer (2 to 4 sentences, recommendation first) -> Options compared (table for 3+) -> Evidence ->
Risks and tradeoffs -> Limits -> Sources. Date saved reports and open with the short answer. Include the requester’s identity only if needed and authorized.

## Confidence vs likelihood (ODNI ICD 203)
ICD 203 separates two things and forbids combining a confidence level and a likelihood term in the same
sentence. [1]
- **Likelihood** = how probable the event or claim is. Use one row of terms consistently: [1]
| almost no chance | very unlikely | unlikely | roughly even chance | likely | very likely | almost certain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-5% | 5-20% | 20-45% | 45-55% | 55-80% | 80-95% | 95-99% |
- **Confidence** = how solid the evidence and reasoning behind the judgement are. I use three levels.

## Confidence rubric (my synthesis of GRADE and ICD 203; label as such)
| Level | When | GRADE analogue [2] |
|---|---|---|
| High | Several independent, good-quality sources (ideally primary) agree; few assumptions; the answer would survive the strongest counter-evidence found | High |
| Medium | Credible sources but thin corroboration, one important assumption, or evidence slightly indirect for this household | Moderate |
| Low | Fragmentary, conflicting or old evidence; rests on assumptions; or only one non-primary source | Low / very low |
Rate down for the same reasons GRADE does: bias, inconsistency, indirectness (study population or
product context differs from ours), imprecision, and selective publication. [2]

## Sentence patterns
- "Recommendation: X. Confidence: medium, because the savings figure comes from one agency estimate at a
  national electricity rate; it would rise to high with your own rate."
- "It is likely (55-80%) that ..." in one sentence; "I have medium confidence in this" in another.
- "I couldn't verify X. I tried A and B; the only source is C, which is ..."
- Conflicts: "Source 1 says $550 a year; source 2 says $600. I use $550 because its assumptions
  (rate, cost, lifespan) are published."

## Fact-check report rules (IFCN + AP)
- Give evidence that supports the claim and evidence that undermines it. [3]
- Give enough sourcing for the reader to replicate the check. [3]
- Quote the exact claim; state the verdict early; then the facts. [4]
- Correct visibly: when an earlier report of mine is wrong, update the same document with a dated
  correction note rather than writing a near-copy. [3]

## Final checklist before sending
1. Answer first, with the recommendation.
2. Every factual claim cited; uncitable claims marked "I couldn't verify this" and listed under
   Limits with what was tried, and the recommendation does not rest on any of them.
3. Every number has its baseline, unit, date and source assumptions.
4. Strongest counter-evidence included.
5. Confidence stated, with the reason and what would change it.
6. Dates checked on anything that changes (prices, incentives, rules, versions).
7. No private household detail in shared report documents.
8. Referral to a professional or emergency route where the question crosses into one.
