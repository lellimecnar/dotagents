# Scoping, estimating, bids, contracts, liens, restarting a stalled job

Source IDs refer to `sources.md`. Items marked (judgment) are practitioner method, not sourced facts.

## 1. Scope before price
- Scope document = plan sheet + room-by-room list + system-by-system list (framing, insulation,
  fireblocking, plumbing fixtures, electrical devices/circuits, HVAC runs, alarms, egress, finishes),
  each line marked OWNER or TRADE, with quantities. (judgment)
- Allowances: where a finish isn't chosen yet, the contract should say who chooses it and how much is
  budgeted (the "allowance"). (judgment)

## 2. Estimating (owner-builder)
- Quantity takeoff from the plan: linear feet of wall -> plates, studs, foam board, fireblocking; wall and
  ceiling area -> drywall, insulation, paint; device and fixture counts from the electrical/plumbing
  layout. Price from current supplier listings and keep the date on every price. (judgment)
- Separate: permit and plan fees (ask the building department; some cities add surcharges such as a
  technology fee on permit and plan-check fees), professional fees (engineer, mitigator), trade bids, owner materials, tool
  rental/purchase, disposal, and a contingency line sized to how much is still unknown (larger when
  walls are closed or the job has stalled). (judgment)
- Show every number with a cheaper alternative and what it trades away, and hand it to the household
  decision-maker; never commit spend.

## 3. Getting and comparing bids (FTC guidance)
- Get multiple written estimates; don't automatically choose the lowest bidder; ask for an explanation
  if there is a big difference among them [S50][S49].
- A written estimate should include a description of the work, materials, completion date and price
  [S50]; FTC's disaster-repair page adds the contractor's contact information [S49].
- Consider only licensed and insured contractors: confirm the licence with the state or county
  government and ask for proof of insurance [S50][S49]. Many jurisdictions also require hired
  structural, plumbing, mechanical, electrical and roofing trades to hold a state and/or local licence
  and be listed on the permit before it issues; check yours.
- Compare bids line by line against your scope: inclusions, exclusions, who pulls the permit, who
  schedules inspections, cleanup, warranty, payment schedule, start/finish dates. (judgment)
- Request bids by email/web form with the scope attached so every bidder prices the same thing and
  there is a written record. (judgment)

## 4. Contract must include
FTC list (contract requirements vary by state; ask for a written contract even where not required):
contractor's name, address, phone and licence number; estimated start and completion date; any promises
made in conversations or calls (scope, labour and material costs); a written statement of the right to
cancel within three business days if signed at home or away from the seller's permanent place of
business; all blank spaces filled in [S50]. A payment schedule [S49]. Don't pay the full amount up front
(some states cap down payments); never make the final payment until the work is done and you're
satisfied [S50][S49].
Also worth requiring (judgment, not FTC-sourced): who obtains permits (FTC flags a contractor asking
the owner to pull permits as a scam sign [S50]); change-order procedure; materials list (brand, model,
size, colour) or allowances; warranties (who honours them, how long, limits); inclusions/exclusions
such as cleanup.

## 5. Mechanics' liens (state law; check your state)
- Unpaid contractors, subs and suppliers can generally lien the property. State law sets the
  notice-of-intent requirements and the deadlines for filing a lien statement, usually counted from the
  claimant's last labor or materials.
- Some states treat a long stop in work (abandonment) as completion for these deadlines, so a stalled
  job can start lien clocks. Check your state's statute.
- Get lien waivers: collect one with each payment from the GC and key subs/suppliers, in the form your
  state requires. (Judgment; attorney for disputes.)

## 6. Restarting a long-stalled project
Restart = re-establish facts, then re-permit, then re-sequence. Do not restart by working.

### 6a. Paperwork inventory
- Approved plan set (version, date, code edition it was drawn to).
- Permit numbers, issue dates, every inspection result and date (look up in the jurisdiction's portal).
- Any correction notices, extension letters, fee receipts, engineer letters, trade permits.
- Contracts, bids, warranties, lien waivers from earlier work.

### 6b. Physical inventory (per room, per system)
For each: installed? inspected and passed? covered since inspection? damaged, wet, moldy, modified?
- Framing: plates PT and anchored; capillary break; fireblocking at top, soffits and 10 ft intervals [S15][S38].
- Insulation: assembly matches building-science rules (foam on concrete, no poly, rim sealed) [S37][S38];
  R-value vs current code [S17].
- Plumbing: drains/vents/supply roughed? test passed? cleanouts accessible?
- Electrical: boxes, circuits, AFCI/GFCI plan, alarm wiring and interconnect [S48][S15].
- Mechanical: supply/return to new rooms; gas changes; combustion air if enclosed [S24].
- Egress: compliant opening per sleeping room and basement; well, ladder, drainage [S15].
- Water: signs of leaks or condensation since work stopped [S42].
- Radon: last test date and level; mitigation present? [S60]
- Materials on hand: list, condition (wet/warped/damaged lumber or drywall gets rejected), still code
  compliant? Tools: what is missing (feeds the kit list). (judgment)

### 6c. Questions for the building department, in writing
See `permits-and-inspections.md` "Restarting with a new permit" (code edition, honouring passed
inspections, reuse of plans, fee, inspection ticket, triggered upgrades).

### 6d. Re-sequence
Rebuild the phase table (`sequencing.md`) starting at the first phase whose hold point is not
documented as passed. Work that was never inspected is treated as not done until the inspector accepts
it, which may mean opening walls.

## 7. Designing around lost tools and scattered materials (judgment)
Treat chronic tool loss as a system problem with a system fix, not a willpower problem:
- One fixed home per tool family (shadow board or labelled clear bins) at the edge of the work zone.
- Phase kits: a bin per phase/block holding exactly that block's tools and consumables; the block starts
  by pulling the kit and ends by returning it. Missing items surface at the start, not mid-task.
- Duplicate cheap, high-loss items (tape measures, pencils, utility knives, bits) and keep one in each kit.
- Materials delivered or bought per phase; a marked staging area per phase; offcuts bin.
- A photo of the kit and the staging area at the end of each block is the reset check.
- These support progress; they never stand in for the permit, the inspection, or a spending decision.
