## Where sources disagree, and what this skill follows
1. **CO alarm location.** The IRC model text places CO alarms outside each separate sleeping area [S15];
   some state statutes set their own placement (e.g. within a set distance of each sleeping-room
   entrance), and manufacturers specify clearances from fuel-burning appliances. Follow: the stricter of
   state statute and adopted code, kept away from fuel appliances per the manufacturer. A statute is
   law; guides are advice.
2. **Smoke alarm distance from cooking.** NFPA public guidance says at least 10 ft [S28]; some fire-service
   handouts say 20 ft. Follow NFPA (the standard-writer) and the alarm's instructions.
3. **New egress window, who may do it.** Local guidance often says a new egress opening needs an
   engineer's design and a licensed contractor, while owner-builder affidavits let owners do their own
   work on a primary residence. Follow the stricter reading (engineer + contractor) unless the building
   department confirms otherwise in writing.
4. **Interior vapor barriers.** Building Science Corp., DOE and ORNL all say no interior polyethylene or
   impermeable finishes on basement walls [S37][S38][S40]; older local practice sometimes used poly.
   Follow the building-science sources; the energy code sets R-value, not the vapor strategy.
5. **GFCI in basements.** 2023 NEC 210.8(A)(5) covers all dwelling basement receptacles [S48]; some states
   amend it back to unfinished basements only, and some let cities be stricter. Follow 2023 NEC unless the
   adopted state or city amendments say otherwise.
6. **Permit expiry/reactivation.** IRC model: invalid if not started within 180 days or suspended 180
   days; building official may extend in writing, 180 days at a time [S18]. Jurisdictions vary widely on
   reactivating expired permits (some charge a reactivation fee, some refuse after 180 days and require a
   new application under current code). The model rule turns on work commencing and not being
   suspended/abandoned, not on inspections; some AHJs use inspections as the evidence of activity.
   Follow: ask the building department in writing; never assume an old permit can be revived.
7. **Egress summaries vs code text.** Plain-language guides often say "all basements and sleeping rooms"
   and show only 5.7 sq ft. The 2024 IRC text has R319.1 exceptions and a 5.0 sq ft grade-floor minimum
   [S15][S21]. Follow the code text plus local amendments; a guide is a summary.
