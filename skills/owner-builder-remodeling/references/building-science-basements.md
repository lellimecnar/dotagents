# Building science for finished basements (moisture, insulation, radon)

Source IDs refer to `sources.md`. Principle: a finished basement must stay dry, be able to dry to the
inside, keep warm humid indoor air off cold concrete, and keep soil gas out [S37][S38].

## 1. Water first
- Bulk water: fix grading, downspouts and leaks before any finish; EPA says moisture problems should be
  addressed before other remodeling work begins [S33].
- Interior insulation is not recommended where the basement has a risk of moisture intrusion (poor
  drainage, high water table), on hollow concrete-block walls unless the cores are fully filled, or where
  there is no capillary break between foundation and wood framing [S40].
- Dry anything wet within 24-48 h and fix the source; target indoor RH about 30-60% [S42].
- Practical pre-check (judgment, not code): look for efflorescence, staining, damp slab edges, rust on
  nails/fasteners in existing framing, and musty odour; if found, diagnose before insulating.

## 2. Wall assembly (cold climate, interior insulation)
Approved approaches in order of risk, per Building Science Corporation and DOE:
1. **Spray foam on the concrete** (closed or open cell): least risky interior approach; must be covered by
   an ignition/thermal barrier [S37][S38][S39].
2. **Rigid foam board fully adhered to the concrete**, seams and edges sealed (acrylic-latex caulk, foam
   sealant or mastic), with a stud wall inside it whose cavity may hold unfaced fiberglass or damp-spray
   cellulose [S38][S39].
3. Foam types and vapor behaviour: up to 2 in unfaced XPS (R-10), 4 in unfaced EPS (R-15), 3 in
   closed-cell spray foam (R-18), or 10 in open-cell (R-35) keep the wall able to dry inward [S37][S38].
   Rigid foam should be unfaced (no foil or plastic facing) for the inward-drying assembly [S38].

Rules:
- **No interior polyethylene, vinyl wallcovering or oil/alkyd/epoxy paint** on the basement wall assembly
  [S37][S40].
- **Rim joist**: insulate with air-impermeable insulation (spray foam or sealed rigid foam) or from the
  exterior; it is the coldest, most condensation-prone spot [S38].
- **Capillary break between concrete and wood**; pressure-treated bottom plate on the slab [S38].
- **Air-tight**: the foam layer must be continuous behind the framed wall so room air cannot reach the
  concrete [S38].
- Use non-paper-faced gypsum below grade and keep drywall at least 1/2 in above the floor [S39][S40].
- Hit the energy-code R-value for your climate zone with this assembly (e.g. zone 5: R-15ci, R-19 cavity, or R-13 + R-5ci) [S17].
  Example: 1 in XPS (~R-5 ci) + R-13 unfaced batt in a 2x4 wall satisfies "13&5ci" (illustration only;
  check product labels).
- Fireblocking still applies to the furred/framed wall (`codes-and-thresholds.md`).

## 3. Slab and floor
- Ideal is rigid foam under the slab with polyethylene directly under the concrete; never a sand layer
  between poly and slab [S37][S38].
- In existing homes rigid foam can go on top of the slab under a floated floor [S39].
- Avoid impermeable floor finishes such as vinyl on a slab that can only dry upward [S38]. (Judgment:
  where a vapor-tolerant floating floor with an underlayment is chosen, follow the flooring maker's slab
  moisture test and limits.)

## 4. Radon in a basement finish
- Test the area before finishing; fixing radon is usually easier and cheaper before the basement is
  finished; test again after the work [S33][S32][S34].
- EPA: fix at >= 4 pCi/L, consider at 2-4 [S30]; base the decision on a long-term test or the average of
  two short-term tests [S31].
- EPA estimates nearly 1 in 15 US homes has elevated radon; levels vary greatly from home to home, so test regardless of neighbours' results [S60].
- Active sub-slab depressurization is the most common, usually most reliable method (50-99%); sealing
  cracks alone is not recommended as a stand-alone technique [S31].
- Plan the finish around the mitigation: leave the suction point and pipe route accessible, keep the
  system's manometer visible, and do not bury the pipe in a wall without a permit-approved route.
  (Judgment.)
- EPA says lowering radon needs technical skill and recommends a trained contractor; if considering a
  DIY install, contact your state radon office first [S60]. A certified mitigator is the default
  recommendation.

## 5. Mechanical and air
- Basements are inside the building boundary and should be dry and conditioned; mechanical systems
  belong in conditioned space [S38].
- Enclosing a gas furnace/water heater: check combustion air volume (50 cu ft per 1,000 Btu/h,
  standard method) [S24].
- New finished rooms need heat supply and return paths. Some AHJs require submittals for new gas lines
  or increased appliance load, and tests of new gas piping or venting; ask.
