# Permits and inspections: owner-builder basics

Source IDs refer to `sources.md`. Rules on who may do work, what needs a permit, fees and permit life
are set by your state and your city or county, and they change. Read your building department's
current pages before acting, and get any decision that matters (expiry, reuse of old inspections,
egress, who may do work) in writing.

## Who is the authority
- Jurisdiction follows the address. A city mailing address is not proof the property is inside city
  limits; it may be county jurisdiction. Confirm with the city or county (parcel lookup or a written
  question) before applying.
- Licensing differs by state. Some states license general contractors and some do not; electricians and
  plumbers are usually licensed by the state, and some cities add their own contractor licence or
  registration. In some states a state board permits and inspects trade work where there is no local
  program; elsewhere the city or county does. Find out which office permits and inspects electrical and
  plumbing work at your address.
- Find the adopted code editions (IRC, IECC, NEC, plumbing/mechanical codes), the local amendments and
  their effective date. A new permit is reviewed under the code in force when it is filed.

## What typically needs a permit for a basement finish
Usually: the basement finish itself (framing new rooms), and electrical, plumbing, mechanical/gas work,
and any new egress opening or structural change. Demolition may need its own permit and, for older
materials, an asbestos survey. Changing the plans after the permit issues usually needs a formal
revision. Like-for-like window replacement in the same opening is often exempt; a new or enlarged
egress opening is not. Check your jurisdiction's permit-exemption list; the IRC model list is in R105.2.

## Who may do the work (varies by state and city)
| Situation | Typical rule (check yours) |
|---|---|
| Owner doing own work on own primary residence | Often allowed as an owner-builder, sometimes with a signed or notarized owner-builder affidavit before the permit issues |
| Owner doing own electrical or plumbing | Allowed only where a state and local homeowner exemption applies; commonly: personally performed, own residence, not for sale or rent, inspected before cover and at completion |
| Anyone hired for structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical or roofing work | Usually must hold the required state and/or local licence and be listed on the permit, with current insurance, before it issues |
| Non-owner running the project | Often must be a licensed or registered general contractor where the jurisdiction licenses them |
| Rental property | Homeowner exemptions often do not apply |
| New egress opening (foundation cut) | Commonly needs an engineer's design and a licensed contractor even where owner-builder work is otherwise allowed; follow the stricter reading unless the building department confirms otherwise in writing |
| Hiring an unlicensed person for licensed trade work | May violate state law, and homeowner insurance may not cover resulting damage |

## Applying
- Use the jurisdiction's residential checklist and forms; many accept applications by email or an
  online portal.
- Plan review can take weeks per round. A complete submittal is the main way to avoid extra rounds.
- Typical basement-finish submittal: scaled floor plan of the whole basement with room uses; electrical
  outlets, smoke and CO alarms, lighting, fans; plumbing changes and cleanouts; furnace and water heater
  location; window sizes and which are egress, with well dimensions and ladder; structural changes
  (posts, beams, joists); areas under 7 ft; insulation values; fixture clearances.
- Fees: get the actual fee from the building department. Some cities add surcharges such as a
  technology fee on permits and plan checks, or a card-payment fee. Work started before the permit
  issues can carry an extra fee (IRC R108.6 model text leaves the amount to the local authority).
- Keep the approved, stamped drawings on site for inspections.

## Inspections
- Most jurisdictions let owners schedule inspections and look up results online; prefer that written
  channel over phone so there is a record.
- Schedule every inspection listed on the permit's inspection ticket; the ticket on the actual permit
  is the authority.
- Typical basement-finish sequence: rough framing (with fireblocking), rough electrical, rough
  plumbing, rough mechanical, insulation, final. Drywall does not go up until the roughs and insulation
  pass. Some jurisdictions add a drywall/fastener or fire inspection.
- Send any affidavits, letters or photos an inspection needs in writing, by the deadline the
  jurisdiction sets.
- Check a contractor's licence with the issuing state or local licensing lookup before hiring.

## Permit life, extensions, expiry
- IRC model R105.5: the permit becomes invalid if authorized work does not commence within 180 days of
  issuance, or is suspended or abandoned for 180 days after it starts; the building official may grant
  written extensions of up to 180 days each, requested in writing before expiry with justifiable cause
  [S18].
- Local rules differ. Some jurisdictions require the permit to be picked up within a set time; some
  treat each passed inspection as evidence of activity (an AHJ practice, not the R105.5 model text);
  some allow only one extension; some refuse to reopen permits expired more than 180 days and require a
  new application under the current code. Some will not issue a new permit while fees are owed on an
  earlier one.
- Separately issued state trade permits (where a state board inspects) can have their own validity
  period and renewal rule.

## Restarting with a new permit: what to ask the building department, in writing
1. Which code edition and local amendments will the new application be reviewed under?
2. Will passed inspections on the expired permit be honoured, or must covered work be exposed or
   re-inspected?
3. Can the old approved plans be resubmitted, or are new drawings needed (e.g., insulation values,
   alarm interconnection, energy code)?
4. Exact fee, and whether any fee credit applies.
5. Which inspections will appear on the new ticket.
6. Any requirement newly triggered (smoke/CO alarm upgrades house-wide; egress; whether any radon
   provisions apply to an alteration or only to new buildings).
7. The new permit's expiration rule: what counts as work commenced, what counts as suspended or
   abandoned, whether inspections are used as the evidence of activity, and how extensions or
   reactivation are requested (in writing).
