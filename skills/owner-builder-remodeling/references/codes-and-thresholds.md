# Codes and thresholds for a basement finish

Model-code values below are from the 2024 IRC unless marked. The 2024 IRC renumbered Chapter 3:
smoke alarms R314 -> R310, CO alarms R315 -> R311, egress R310 -> R319, room area R304 -> R312,
ceiling height R305 -> R313 [S15][S20][S21]. Old plans and correction notices may cite the 2021 numbers.
Always confirm the edition and local amendments the permit is reviewed under. Source IDs refer to
`sources.md`.

## Emergency escape and rescue openings (2024 IRC R319; 2021 R310)
| Item | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Where required (default) | Basements, habitable attics, the room a sleeping loft opens to, and every sleeping room: at least one operable opening (window or exterior door); where a basement has sleeping rooms, one in each sleeping room. Subject to the R319.1 exceptions (e.g. Exception 3: sprinklered dwelling per P2904 -> basement sleeping rooms exempt if the basement has one R318 means of egress + one escape opening, or two R318 means of egress) and to local amendments | [S15][S21] |
| Net clear opening | >= 5.7 sq ft, except a grade-floor opening, whose minimum is 5.0 sq ft (R319.2.1 Exception). One threshold applies per opening: below-grade basement openings use 5.7 | [S15][S21] |
| Clear opening height | >= 24 in | [S15][S21] |
| Clear opening width | >= 20 in | [S15][S21] |
| Sill height | <= 44 in above finished floor | [S15][S21] |
| Operation | From inside, without tools, keys or special knowledge | [S15] |
| Window well | >= 9 sq ft, min. 36 in dimension, window must open fully | [S15] |
| Well ladder | Permanently affixed ladder if well > 44 in deep; may encroach <= 6 in | [S15] |
| Well steps (alternative) | >= 12 in wide, >= 5 in tread, <= 18 in riser | [S15] (R319.4.2.2 text) |
| New egress opening (local practice) | Many jurisdictions require an engineer's design for the foundation cut, well drainage, a licensed contractor and a permit; check yours | - |

Illustration (arithmetic, 5.7 sq ft case): a 24 in clear height needs about 34.2 in clear width; a
20 in clear width needs about 41 in clear height.

## Carbon monoxide alarms (2024 IRC R311; 2021 R315; state law where it exists)
| Item | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Code trigger (2024 IRC R311.2.2 model) | Permitted alteration/repair/addition -> CO alarms as for new dwellings; exceptions: exterior-surface work, plumbing, and mechanical work that is not fuel-fired (fuel-fired mechanical work is NOT exempt). New-construction CO rule applies only with a fuel-fired appliance or an attached garage communicating with the dwelling. Verify the locally adopted edition/amendments | [S22] |
| State law trigger (where one exists) | Some states require CO alarms when permitted interior alterations, repairs, fuel-fired appliance replacements or additions occur, or a sleeping room is added, often only in dwellings with fuel-fired appliances or an attached garage, and often with a placement rule (e.g. within a set distance of each sleeping-room entrance). Check your state statute; follow whichever of statute and code is stricter | - |
| Placement | Outside each sleeping area and per the alarm manufacturer, kept away from fuel-burning appliances per the instructions | [S15] |

## Fireblocking (2024 IRC R302.11)
| Item | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Furred/framed walls against foundation | Fireblock concealed spaces at 10 ft intervals along the wall; fireblock at top (and soffits) | [S15] (R302.11) |
| Gaps at top and bottom | Fill with securely retained mineral wool or glass-fiber batts (an approved material) | [S16] |
| Approved materials | 2 in nominal lumber; two layers 1 in lumber with broken laps; 23/32 in wood structural panel with backed joints; 3/4 in particleboard with backed joints; 1/2 in gypsum board; 1/4 in cement-based millboard; mineral wool or glass-fiber batts securely retained; cellulose as tested (ASTM E119/UL 263) | [S16] |
| Practical | Install fireblocking as the framing goes up, before the wall is closed (judgment) | - |

## Framing details
- Pressure-treated (decay-resistant) bottom plate anchored to the slab, with a capillary break [S38].
- Where soils move (expansive soils), some AHJs require floating/slip partitions that leave a gap
  between the wall and the slab or floor framing; follow the AHJ's published detail. (Judgment: ask.)

## Ceiling height (2024 IRC R313; 2021 R305)
| Space | Minimum | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Habitable space, hallways | 7 ft | [S23] |
| Bathrooms, toilet rooms, laundry | 6 ft 8 in | [S23] |
| Shower/tub with showerhead | 6 ft 8 in over 30 x 30 in at the showerhead | [S23] |
| Beams, girders, ducts in basement with habitable space | May project to 6 ft 4 in above finished floor | [S23] |
| Non-habitable basement portions | 6 ft 8 in; obstructions to 6 ft 4 in | [S23] |
| Any area finishing under 7 ft | Ask the building department before designing it as habitable (judgment) | - |
(2021 text; confirm the 2024 R313 wording with the AHJ.)

## Existing buildings (2024 IRC model text; check the local adoption and amendments)
- **Ceiling height:** In the 2024 model text, new habitable space in an existing basement or a new habitable attic in an existing building generally requires 6 ft 8 in; bathrooms, toilet rooms and laundry rooms require 6 ft 4 in. Read R313.1.2 for sloped-ceiling and obstruction exceptions.
- **Egress:** New sleeping rooms in an existing basement need an emergency escape and rescue opening; other basement alterations or repairs do not trigger that requirement under R319.7. Check R319.7.1 before relying on an existing or replacement window.
- **Insulation:** When unconditioned below-grade space becomes conditioned, insulate the envelope walls enclosing it. For already conditioned below-grade space, the provision applies when those envelope walls are altered. Check N1111.1.1.5 and the applicable N1102.1 requirements.
- Local handouts often print new-construction or older-edition values: design to the adopted code text,
  check amendments, and get the building department's written agreement before relying on an
  existing-building exception.
Source: ICC public-access 2024 IRC, chapters 3 and 11 (codes.iccsafe.org), checked November 2026.

## Minimum room size
Habitable rooms (incl. bedrooms) >= 70 sq ft and no horizontal dimension under 7 ft (2021 R304 /
2024 R312) [S20].

## Insulation (2024 IRC Table N1102.1.3, R-value path)
| Climate zone | Basement wall R-value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | R-15 continuous, or R-19 cavity, or R-13 cavity + R-5 continuous | [S17] |
| 4 (not marine) | R-10ci or R-13 | [S17] |
| 6-8 | R-15ci or R-19 or R-13 + R-5ci | [S17] |
Look up your county's IECC climate zone [S25], then confirm which energy code your state or city has
adopted and any amendments; adopted energy codes can differ from the model IECC. The assembly (where the
foam goes, no poly) comes from `building-science-basements.md`.

Foam plastic must be covered: codes typically require a thermal barrier (typically 1/2 in gypsum) or an
ignition barrier (typically 3/8 in gypsum) depending on location, unless the product is tested for
exposure [S41]; DOE says spray foam must be covered with ignition-barrier paint or 1/2 in gypsum [S39].

## Combustion air when enclosing fuel-burning appliances (IRC G2407 / IFGC 304)
- Standard method: room volume >= 50 cu ft per 1,000 Btu/h of the total input of all appliances in the
  space [S24].
- Where infiltration is known to be <= 0.40 ACH, the Known Air Infiltration Rate method must be used [S24].
- Example: a 50,000 Btu/h water heater needs 2,500 cu ft of indoor air volume [S24]. If the enclosed
  room is smaller, openings to other rooms or outdoors are required. Direct-vent sealed-combustion
  appliances change the math; have the mechanical inspector or an HVAC pro confirm.

## Radon
| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EPA action level | Fix at >= 4 pCi/L (150 Bq/m3); consider fixing at 2-4 pCi/L | [S30] |
| Decision basis | One long-term test, or the average of two short-term tests | [S31] |
| Achievable | Most homes can be reduced to 2 pCi/L or below | [S31] |
| US prevalence | Nearly 1 in 15 US homes is estimated to have elevated radon; levels vary home to home, so test | [S60] |
| Short-term test (EPA) | 2-90 days; for a 2-3 day test close windows and outside doors 12 h before and during; lowest lived-in level; >= 20 in above the floor, away from drafts and exterior walls | [S60] |
| Long-term test | More than 90 days | [S60] |
| Mitigation cost | EPA: most homes can be fixed for about the cost of other common home repairs; get estimates | [S60] |
| Sub-slab depressurization | 50-99% reduction; most common and usually most reliable | [S31] |
| Retest a mitigated home | Every two years (EPA) | [S60] |
| Renovation | Test before converting a basement to living space; test again after the work | [S60] |
| Code | 2024 IRC Appendix BE (radon-resistant construction) applies only where adopted, usually for new buildings; ask whether anything applies to an alteration | [S15] |

## Moisture
Dry wet materials within 24-48 hours and fix the water source; keep indoor RH about 30-60% [S42].
