# Sources: where to look, in order, and how much each one weighs

All Church-published material below lives on churchofjesuschrist.org (Gospel Library, `/study/...`). Look up
URLs by searching; never build one from memory without fetching it.

## 1. Canonized scripture (highest authority)
- The standard works: Holy Bible (King James Version, Latter-day Saint edition), Book of Mormon, Doctrine and
  Covenants, Pearl of Great Price. Includes the Official Declarations in the D&C.
- Study helps in the Latter-day Saint edition (chapter headings, footnotes, Topical Guide, Bible Dictionary, Guide
  to the Scriptures, Joseph Smith Translation excerpts) are helpful Church publications but are not canon; label
  them as study helps.
- Original-language and textual context for the Bible: standard scholarly lexicons and translations, labelled as
  scholarship. Book of Mormon textual history: the Book of Mormon critical text project and the Joseph Smith Papers
  manuscript images.

## 2. Official statements and current Church teaching
- Proclamations and declarations issued by the First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve, and First Presidency
  statements.
- General conference addresses (search by topic, speaker or year in the Gospel Library). The current prophet's and
  apostles' teachings carry the most weight for current practice; each talk is still one speaker's address.
- General Handbook: current policy and procedure. Policy is not the same as doctrine; label it policy and give the
  section number.
- Gospel Topics, Gospel Topics Essays, Church History Topics, Newsroom official statements.
- Manuals: Come, Follow Me; Teachings of Presidents of the Church; Institute and Seminary manuals; Preach My Gospel;
  Teaching in the Savior's Way; True to the Faith; Guide to the Scriptures.
- Church magazines: Liahona (and its predecessors Ensign, New Era, Improvement Era, etc.), For the Strength of
  Youth, Friend. Say which magazine and issue.
- Church history: the multi-volume Saints narrative history, Revelations in Context, Church History Library
  catalog, and the Joseph Smith Papers (the primary documents themselves, with editorial notes).

## 3. Faithful scholarship (explains and contextualizes; not doctrine)
- BYU Religious Studies Center publications, BYU Studies Quarterly, Neal A. Maxwell Institute (including the
  Journal of Book of Mormon Studies), Interpreter: A Journal of Latter-day Saint Faith and Scholarship, Scripture
  Central (formerly Book of Mormon Central), FAIR (FAIRLatterDaySaints), BYU Scripture Citation Index
  (which talks cite a verse), BYU devotionals and education-week talks.
- Weigh by argument and evidence, not by venue. Say when faithful scholars disagree with each other.

## 4. Independent academic and critical sources
- Journal of Mormon History, Dialogue, university-press histories, non-Latter-day Saint scholarship.
- Critics' own writings when the question is about their argument: quote them from their source, then answer with
  the best sources above. Never characterize an argument you have not read.

## Historical sermons and secondary compilations (use with care)
- Journal of Discourses, History of the Church, older compilations (e.g. Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith,
  Doctrines of Salvation, Mormon Doctrine): valuable history, not canon. Some were transcribed or edited by others;
  check the Joseph Smith Papers or the original manuscript where it matters, and say the source's limits.
- Anonymous quote sites, memes, social posts, and "a General Authority once said" claims: never a source. Find
  the original or report the quote as unverified.

## Weighing authority (how to label a teaching)
Use the Church's own guidance on what constitutes doctrine: search the Newsroom statement "Approaching Mormon
Doctrine" and Elder D. Todd Christofferson's conference address "The Doctrine of Christ" and read them before
labelling a contested teaching. Labels to use:
- **Scripture**: in the standard works.
- **Official doctrine**: taught consistently by the First Presidency and Twelve together, in councils, declarations
  or proclamations, and consistent with scripture.
- **Current policy**: General Handbook or official policy announcement (can change).
- **Leader's teaching**: one leader's talk or writing; strong guidance, give speaker and date.
- **Manual or magazine explanation**: Church-approved teaching material.
- **Scholarship**: an argument by a scholar; give the author.
- **Speculation or folk belief**: not taught officially; say so plainly.
When teachings changed over time, show the sequence with dates rather than flattening it.
