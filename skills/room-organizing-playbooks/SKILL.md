# Room-by-room organizing playbooks

## Purpose
Give a playbook for each room or zone of a family home (sequence, zones, rules, realistic time,
what goes wrong, maintenance) and the storage-safety facts that matter while organizing:
moisture, fire egress and clearances, chemicals, batteries, tip-over and shelf loads.

Researched 2026-11-09. Tags: [NFPA] [CPSC] [EPA] [EXT] [NARA] [NIOSH] = fact came from that opened source (see Sources). "(judgment)" = practitioner rule of thumb, not a sourced fact. Times are planning estimates for one adult plus one helper, not measured data.

## When to use
- Guiding someone through a kitchen, pantry, closet, kids' room, toys, bathroom, linen, laundry,
  entryway, home office, garage, basement, attic, holiday decor, or tool/hobby area.
- Any time storage safety is involved (batteries, flammables, egress, furniture tip-over, loads).

## Method

## 0. Universal method (use for every room)

1. **Safety sweep first (10 min).** Before any sorting, clear exits and walkways, move anything combustible 3 ft from the furnace, and bag loose batteries (section 17). [NFPA]
2. **Empty one zone at a time, never the whole room.** A zone is one drawer, one shelf, one closet. Finish it before starting the next. (judgment)
3. **Sort into four: Keep / Donate-Sell / Recycle-Hazardous / Trash.** Skip a "maybe" pile; use a dated "decide later" box (90 days, then donate unopened). (judgment; AARP piece uses keep/donate/toss and "one home for everything, like with like, something in, something out")
4. **Move outgoing items out the same day.** Trash and recycling leave the house the same day; donations go in the car trunk the same day. Disposal routing is in the sibling skill `unwanted-item-routing`.
5. **Assign homes, then buy containers.** Measure after the cull. Buying bins first locks in the clutter. (judgment)
6. **Label so a child can use it** (picture labels for under-7s).
7. **Maintenance hook per room:** a 10-minute weekly reset plus a seasonal 30-minute check. Each playbook below names its trigger.

Sentimental items go last in every room; ask possible heirs before passing things on and accept a no. (AARP)

### Realistic effort (planning estimates, judgment)
Plan sessions of 3 to 4 hours max. Typical: closet 1-2 h; bathroom 1-2 h; kitchen 4-8 h across two sessions; kids' room 3-5 h with the child present; garage or basement 1-3 full days; attic 1-2 days. Whole-house projects run weeks, not a weekend.

### Default order of attack (adjust to the household's priorities)
1. Safety items (batteries, flammables, tip-over anchors, exits) in all rooms: one afternoon.
2. Storage and staging areas that block other projects (garage, basement), so nothing is stored twice.
3. The paper hub / home office (`household-paper-flow`).
4. Kids' rooms and toys, with the children present, on a school break.
5. Daily-life quick wins: kitchen/pantry, bathrooms, linen, laundry, entryway.
6. Seasonal decor last, when it is next packed away.

## 16. Furniture tip-over, shelves and weight (cross-room)

- Anchor TVs and tall or top-heavy furniture with anti-tip brackets and follow manufacturer instructions; use TV stands rated for the TV; recycle old CRT TVs rather than keep them in use. [CPSC]
- Do the dressers and bookcases in every kids' room first; then the basement and garage shelving.
- Heavy items low; light items high; known weight limits on every shelf; never exceed the rating. Overhead and wall shelves: stud or joist anchoring only; drywall anchors are not enough for heavy loads (judgment).
- Rental or hollow-wall home: use manufacturer-approved wall anchors and ask the landlord; confirm stud location with a stud finder.
- **Fitting storage to a space (judgment):** measure the real usable space: width, depth left after doors, hinges and door-mounted hooks or pegboard, and height between shelves. Before recommending a bin, box or machine, read the manufacturer's exterior dimensions on its spec sheet and allow for lids, handles and moving parts. Equipment with moving parts needs its full range of travel, not just its footprint: move each axis to both ends and measure. Confirm which items will stay, move or be removed before designing storage around them.
- Overhead racks and heavy shelving: follow that product's manual for joist size, spacing, anchoring and load limit; confirm joist size and direction in the actual ceiling before recommending a rack, and stay well under the rated load.

## 17. Hazardous and fire-risk storage (cross-room checklist)

**Batteries [NFPA; EPA; CPSC]**
- Lithium-ion: buy listed products, use the cord that came with the device, charge on hard flat surfaces, unplug when full, charge e-bikes, scooters and power tools outside, never charge under pillows.
- Store in temperature-controlled spots, out of damp places, away from anything that can burn; keep e-bikes and scooters out of exits and hallways. [NFPA]
- Used batteries: bag each in its own plastic bag, tape the terminals; do not put in trash or curbside recycling; do not pile. Damaged or swollen ones: call the manufacturer; if one starts smoking or heating, get out and call 911. [EPA; NFPA]
- Button/coin batteries: out of kids' reach and secure compartments. [CPSC]
**Paint, solvents, chemicals [EPA; NFPA]**: original labeled containers, never food containers, never mixed, away from heat, in a locked or high place away from children and pets; do not store in glass; keep paint from freezing (judgment; check the label).
**Flammable liquids [CPSC; NFPA]**: see section 11. Rags with oil or solvent: sealed metal can or dry outdoors.
**Propane:** grill-size tanks go to the county hazardous waste facility; do not store indoors (judgment).
**Medicines and sharps [FDA]**: see section 6.
**Fire extinguisher (judgment):** one ABC unit in the kitchen and one in the garage, near the exit.
**Egress and alarms [NFPA]:** two ways out of every room if possible, practiced plan, alarms on every level including basement and inside and outside each sleeping area.

## 18. Maintenance calendar

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| Weekly | 10-minute reset of every zone; process paper tray |
| Monthly | Humidity/sump check; test alarms; medicine and pantry spot-check |
| Quarterly | Dryer vent clean; closet in-out; toy rotation |
| Spring and fall | Garage and basement reset; overhead load check; wardrobe swap |
| Annual | Furnace and chimney service by a licensed pro; replace alarms per label; holiday pack-away audit; radon retest every 2 years if mitigated |

## Pitfalls
- Buying bins before culling and measuring.
- Emptying a whole room at once instead of one zone.
- Giving a vague zone summary when the person needs a concrete per-cabinet map; inventing current
  contents instead of asking a short question.
- Storing paper, photos or fabric in damp basements, garages or hot attics.
- Treating unverified figures (shelf ratings from retail snippets, egress dimensions) as fact.

## Verification
- Safety sweep done before sorting; exits clear; batteries bagged; tall furniture anchored.
- Every kept item has a home; outgoing items left the house the same day.
- Each room has a named maintenance trigger.
