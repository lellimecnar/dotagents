# Room playbooks: storage and hobby areas

## 11. Garage

**Steps:** zone map on the floor with tape -> remove trash/hazard items (section 17) -> big movers (bikes, sports, yard) -> shelving -> wall systems -> overhead.
**Zones:** vehicle clearance (keep walkable around cars); yard/garden; sports and bikes; tools and workbench; paint/chemicals (locked, high, cool); seasonal overhead; recycle/donate staging.
**Safety:**
- Do not store gasoline or gasoline equipment inside the house, utility room or garage where vapors could meet a spark or flame; gas appliances such as dryers, furnaces and older water heaters can ignite vapors. [CPSC] NFPA says if you keep gasoline, use a listed, labeled container and store it outside the home in a garage or shed, never the basement, never indoors even in small amounts. [NFPA] The two sources differ; use the stricter reading: a detached shed if possible, and the smallest quantity in a listed can otherwise.
- Keep oil-based paint and solvents in original, tightly capped containers, never glass, away from heat, sparks and motors. [NFPA]
- Gas water heaters in garages: older CPSC briefing text (search snippet, not opened) cited burners at least 18 in above the floor; verify with a licensed plumber and current code. (unverified)
- Overhead racks: weight limits assume proper anchoring into joists (one rack manufacturer's manual assumed at least 2x6 joists at 24 in on center or closer and anchoring to at least 2 joists per corner; limits 300-600 lb depending on model); spread loads, never climb on it; stop if the ceiling's integrity is in doubt. Do not store heavy tools, chemicals or fragile items overhead; use it for seasonal bins and luggage. [rack manual; garageliving.com]
- Do not let the rack or contents touch a garage door in motion (the manual warns of items that could contact the racks, such as doors). 
**Time:** 1-3 days. **Wrong:** storing paint and batteries where it freezes or bakes; shelves unanchored; no vehicle clearance. **Maintenance:** spring and fall 1-hour reset; overhead load check twice a year.

## 12. Basement (including during a renovation)

**Before you store anything:**
1. **Water check.** Fix gutters, grade soil away from the foundation, check drain lines; dry anything wet within 24-48 hours to prevent mold; keep humidity below 60 percent (ideally 30-50) with a meter and dehumidifier. [EPA]
2. **Radon.** Check the EPA radon zone for the area; some regions average several times the national indoor level. Act at 4 pCi/L; test the lowest livable level; retest when you start living or sleeping in the basement or after a major renovation. Short tests run 3-7 days. Mitigation is usually sub-slab depressurization by a certified pro. [EXT]
3. **Egress and alarms.** Smoke and CO alarms on every level including the basement. [NFPA] A basement bedroom needs an emergency escape and rescue opening. Model-code figures from an ICC snippet (not opened): minimum net clear opening 5.7 sq ft, at least 24 in high and 20 in wide, sill no higher than 44 in, window well if below grade. The locally adopted code and amendments govern; confirm with the local building division and your contractor before finishing the space. (unverified)
4. **Keep clear:** furnace, water heater and electrical panel; things that can burn at least 3 ft from the furnace; keep the area clean. [NFPA]
5. **FEMA principle:** put valuables and important papers in waterproof containers above likely flood level, ideally on an upper floor; sealed bins supplement elevation, they do not replace it (from a FEMA search summary; brochure was too large to open). Insurance note: homeowners policies typically exclude flood. [ready.gov]

**Storage rules:** nothing directly on the concrete: put shelving on feet or pallets with a 4-6 in gap (judgment); closed plastic bins with latching lids, not cardboard; no papers, photos or fabric (NARA lists damp basements, garages and hot attics as places to avoid for family papers) [NARA]; keep a printed inventory per bin; do not store gasoline in the basement. [NFPA]
**Zones for a project basement:** staging (materials, away from exits), tool bay, donation/trash outbox, and a "do not store" rule for anything not needed until after the build.
**Time:** 1-3 days to declutter before construction. **Maintenance:** check humidity and sump monthly, bins twice a year after spring melt and summer storms.

## 13. Attic (and crawl spaces)

**Steps:** headlamp and mask -> inventory by bin -> keep only what survives temperature swings -> label -> map.
**Rules:** attics bake and freeze; avoid storing photos, papers, candles, electronics, batteries or anything melt-prone there. [NARA]; do not block eave vents or insulation; walk only on joists; keep hatch access clear; do not load beyond what framing carries (ask a pro; judgment).
**Time:** 1-2 days. **Maintenance:** yearly look for leaks, pests and moisture stains.

## 14. Holiday decorations

**Steps (do it when packing away):** test every light string; discard damaged; keep one tote per holiday, labeled by room; photograph the tree set-up.
**Safety [NFPA]:** buy testing-lab-listed string lights; replace worn cords; follow the manufacturer's strand limit; hang with clips, not nails; turn lights off at bedtime; trees at least 3 ft from heat; do not block an exit; water live trees daily and remove when dry; never leave a dried tree in the garage; keep exits clear of decorations; use battery candles.
**Storage:** overhead rack or high garage shelf in sealed totes (attic or basement floor only with moisture control).
**Time:** 1-2 h to pack. **Wrong:** tangled lights reused at all cost; keeping 4 sets of the same ornaments. **Maintenance:** annual pack-away audit.

## 15. Tools and hobby supplies (workshop, crafts, 3D printing)

**General:** one tool, one home; a shadow board or labeled drawer for hand tools; consumables (blades, bits) in labeled small bins; power-tool batteries stored per section 17.
**3D printing [NIOSH; CPSC via search]:**
- Prefer PLA over ABS where possible (a cited study found ABS gave 3-4 times higher emissions); ventilate; do not print in the bedroom or office area; avoid being near the printer at startup and after failures; clean with solvents only with ventilation and gloves. [NIOSH; search summary]
- Fire: unattended printing, thermal runaway and fan failure are the risks; put a smoke alarm in the room and use listed equipment; print only when someone is home. (CPSC via search snippet; not opened)
- Filament: keep dry in sealed bags with desiccant (judgment, not sourced); keep resin, IPA and cleaning solvents in original containers away from heat; dispose of liquid resin as hazardous waste (see `unwanted-item-routing`).
- Printer on a non-combustible, stable surface, 3 ft from combustibles; one shelf for spools.
- **Placing a big printer:** before giving a cabinet a theme, ask which printers stay and which get sold, because the set of printers changes the whole map. Bed-slingers need roughly frame depth plus bed length in depth (estimate; jog Y to both ends and measure). Rule out benches under wall cabinets, cabinet tops near the ceiling, desks and HVAC vents. Never use a switched outlet or a power bar shared with lights. Use a rigid, low stand, and keep doors closed around small kids (open frame, hot nozzle).
**Craft:** clear bins by medium; paints that have frozen are unusable. **Time:** 2-4 h. **Maintenance:** monthly "use it or lose it" 10-minute pass.
