# Work methods for a founder with ADHD

Scope: work design and accountability only. Never advise on diagnosis, medication or treatment; that belongs to the person's clinician.

Sources:
- [LVT] Lerner, Verheul & Thurik (2018), "Entrepreneurship and attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a large-scale study involving the clinical condition of ADHD", Small Business Economics: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11187-018-0061-1
- [WYTM] Wiklund, Yu, Tucker & Marino (2017), "ADHD, impulsivity and entrepreneurship", Journal of Business Venturing 32(6): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusvent.2017.07.002
- [TRAN] Tran, Wiklund, Antshel, Jhawar & Montgomery (2026), "Entrepreneurship and ADHD: A Meta-Analytical Assessment...", Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice 50(3): https://doi.org/10.1177/10422587251392498
- [GS] Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), "Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis": https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1
- [CH-TD] CHADD, Time Management and ADHD: To-Do Lists: https://chadd.org/for-adults/time-management-and-adhd-to-do-lists
- [CH-PL] CHADD, Time Management and ADHD: Day Planners: https://chadd.org/for-adults/time-management-planner
- [CH-CO] CHADD, Evidence-Based Coaching for Adults with ADHD: https://chadd.org/adhd-news/adhd-news-adults/evidence-based-coaching-for-adults-with-adhd

## What the research says
| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| In N = 9,869, clinical ADHD was positively connected with entrepreneurial intentions and with entrepreneurial action (cross-sectional) | [LVT] |
| Inattention is negatively and hyperactivity positively associated with entrepreneurship; sensation seeking and lack of premeditation push toward it, urgency against | [WYTM] |
| Meta-analysis of 298 effect sizes from 47 studies: hyperactivity/impulsivity relates positively to entrepreneurial attitudes and behaviours but not to post-launch outcomes; inattention relates negatively to post-launch outcomes; total symptoms and diagnosis relate positively to entrepreneurial behaviour yet negatively to outcomes | [TRAN] |
| If-then plans ("If situation Y, then I will do X") had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment, d = .65, across 94 tests (general population, not ADHD-specific); they help starting, shielding from distraction, and disengaging from failing actions | [GS] |

**Where narratives and data differ:** popular accounts call ADHD an entrepreneurial "superpower". The meta-analysis [TRAN] says it helps people start ventures but is linked to worse outcomes after launch, mainly through inattention. Followed here: assume starting energy is available and design all structure around follow-through, finishing and routine work (books, follow-ups, customer support).

## Methods that follow
| Method | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Specific, physical actions | List actions you can do on reading them, not vague items ("exercise" gets skipped) | [CH-TD] |
| Break projects into tasks | Short-term lists hold tasks, not projects; do-able in one sitting | [CH-TD] |
| Top three | When a list is long, number the three most important | [CH-TD] |
| Smallest first step | Coaches break tasks into chunks in behavioural language and identify the smallest first step | [CH-CO] |
| Lower expectations per block | Realistic expectations and external supports | [CH-CO] |
| External accountability | Ask a spouse, friend or coworker to be a "planner coach" to prompt or receive reports | [CH-PL] |
| Daily planning session | Fixed time each day to plan the next day; check the planner morning, midday, evening | [CH-PL] |
| Implementation intentions | Write each key task as "If/When [time, place, cue], then I will [action]" | [GS] |
| Capture list | Brain-dump list, transferred to the short action list every day or two | [CH-TD] |

## How this skill applies them (working rules)
- Hand over 1-3 actions, already ordered, each named by its concrete result, each under about 90 minutes; never a start-up step.
- Every action has a cue and a day: "Monday after the first coffee, write 10 prospect names in the sheet."
- Name the accountability partner and the check-in channel (written check-ins are fine).
- Ship in increments that a customer can see within a week; end every work block with something sent, published or asked, not only "progress".
- Batch the boring follow-through (books, invoices, follow-up messages) into one short, fixed weekly slot with a checklist; automate where a built-in tool exists.
- Two-week experiments with a written end date beat open-ended projects: the end date forces the keep/change/kill decision.
- Keep options few; sorting is the advisor's job, not the founder's.
