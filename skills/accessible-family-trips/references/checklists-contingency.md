# Checklists and contingency plans

Checked 2026-11-14. Medical items here are planning prompts, not medical advice; the member's clinician decides anything clinical.

## A. Outing checklist (day trips)
1. Venue page re-checked today: hours, alerts, closures, reservation/ticket needed, last entry. Note the check date.
2. Access route verified end to end: accessible parking -> path -> main experience -> accessible restroom (open this season). Grades, surfaces, approach barriers noted.
3. Sensory plan: social story / venue social narrative or virtual tour previewed; headphones and comfort items packed; quiet spot identified; visual schedule; the exit plan ("we can leave any time; leaving early still counts").
4. Supervision plan for wandering risk (Section D) and water/road hazards on the route.
5. Weather for the time window, sun, cold; layers; water and snacks (food plan priced).
6. Go-bag (Section C).
7. Timing fits the household's fixed anchors with buffer; no outing on a travel-heavy day; downtime built in. Practitioner rule: keep activities to about four hours or less including down-time (SATH, https://sath.org/the-ten-golden-rules-of-autistic-travel).
8. Fallback: indoor or in-car option nearby if the day goes badly.

- Airlines can still refuse carriage if their medical department judges a passenger unfit; a clinician letter is advice to the airline, not a guarantee. Frequent travelers with stable conditions can ask airlines about a FREMEC number (CDC chronic illness chapter).
- International trips: check destination medication restrictions with the embassy; enroll in STEP; consider travel medical and evacuation insurance (https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/disability).

## C. Medical go-bag (adapt per member)
- All daily and rescue medications plus extra doses for delay, in carry-on; never in a checked bag or hot car trunk (CDC; SATH).
- Copies: clinician letter, medication list, allergies, insurance cards, emergency contacts, recent photo of each child (SATH recommends a photo taken the same day to describe clothing).
- Medical alert ID or wearable ID; for children with tactile sensitivity, shoe IDs or temporary tattoos for outings (National Autism Association, https://nationalautismassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/NAA_Wandering-Brochure_0218.pdf).
- Device chargers/batteries; spare glasses; toileting/changing supplies; comfort and sensory items; first-aid basics; sun protection.

## D. Wandering / elopement plan
Source: National Autism Association, https://nationalautismassociation.org/resources/wandering
- Nearly half of children with autism tend to wander/bolt; more than a third of those who wander rarely or never can tell their name/address/phone; drowning accounts for 71% of lethal outcomes, traffic 18% (NAA figures, citing Pediatrics and NAA studies).
- Highest-risk times: transitions, commotion, holidays, vacations, unfamiliar settings, outings, camping/hiking.
- Before the outing: wearable ID; current photo; identify triggers (noise, lights); bring calming tools; pick routes away from open water where possible.
- During: **"Tag, you're it"** - name one adult as the sole supervisor for an agreed time block and hand off out loud.
- If someone is missing: contact local emergency services immediately, flag nearby water and roads, and follow responders’ instructions.
- At hotels/rentals: door chimes or portable alarms; ask for rooms away from pools/exits when possible (practitioner judgment; NAA recommends door/window chimes at home).

## E. Altitude
Source: CDC Yellow Book 2026, https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html
- Any unacclimatized traveler **sleeping at 8,000 ft (2,450 m) or higher**, sometimes lower, is at risk; children are as susceptible as adults; fitness does not protect.
- **Day trips with an evening return to lower altitude are much less stressful** than sleeping high. Many mountain resort towns sit at or above 8,000 ft; check the lodging's elevation, not just the area's.
- Wilderness Medical Society guidance (via CDC): avoid going to a sleeping altitude of 9,000 ft or more in a single day; above 9,800 ft, raise sleeping altitude no more than ~1,650 ft per night; 2-3 nights at 8,000-9,000 ft before going higher is markedly protective.
- Travelers with compromised lung function, and anyone on respiratory depressants, need clinician input before high-altitude stays.
- Worsening symptoms, confusion, or breathlessness at rest: descend and get emergency care.
- Note: the Yellow Book is written for international travel; its altitude physiology applies equally to domestic mountain trips.

## F. Sensory-friendly planning
- Prefer venues with: Certified Autism Center status (80%+ staff trained); KultureCity Sensory Inclusive certification (staff training, sensory bags, quiet areas, app with social stories, https://lsc.org/news-and-social/news/lsc-kulture-city); scheduled sensory-friendly sessions (lower sound, more light, capped crowds).
- Use the venue's social narrative/sensory map; preview virtual tours; build a visual schedule that ends with "all done."
- Schedule against crowds: avoid free days and peak holiday nights for crowd-sensitive members; choose early opening or value weeknights.
- Never plan sightseeing on travel days; build midday and evening down-time; stay flexible (SATH, https://sath.org/the-ten-golden-rules-of-autistic-travel).
- Pre-flight: Wings for All airport rehearsal (https://thearc.org/our-initiatives/travel/); TSA Cares PSS; preboarding to settle in.
- Amusement parks: the free IBCCES Accessibility Card is accepted at many parks for accommodation requests (https://nncil.org/accessible-travel, practitioner; verify with the park).

## G. Emergency and escalation
- Life-threatening emergency or missing person: contact local emergency services immediately (911 in the U.S.).
- Medical questions: the member's clinician (or nurse line through their insurer); you prepare questions, not answers.
- Rights violations: written complaint routes in accessibility-rights.md.
- Venue-specific help: use the venue's accessibility email for accommodation requests and keep the written reply.
