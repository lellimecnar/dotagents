# Budget methods

Prices, dates and rules change; look them up on the source page at planning time and state the check date in any proposal.

## Price the whole party
- Always compute all-in for every member by age tier (adult/youth/child/under-2), plus fees, parking, fuel, food, rentals. Compare all-in fare with the all-in budget.
- Age tiers differ by venue and by pass. Example: federal passes admit children 15 and under free at per-person sites, while some museums are free for everyone 18 and under.

## Federal: America the Beautiful passes
Source: https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm (current prices are there; quote them with the check date)

| Pass | Who |
|---|---|
| Annual (resident / non-resident pricing may differ) | anyone; check residency rules |
| Senior Annual / Lifetime | older U.S. citizens/residents; check the age rule |
| Military Annual / Lifetime | current military + dependents / Gold Star families, veterans |
| **Access Pass** (lifetime) | U.S. citizens/residents with a permanent disability |
| 4th Grade Pass | U.S. 4th graders |
| Volunteer | earned by volunteer service hours |

Prices (including which passes are free): look up on the passes page above and quote with the check date.

- Covers **entrance fees and standard amenity (day-use) fees** at NPS, USFWS, USFS, BLM, Reclamation and USACE sites: one private vehicle at per-vehicle sites, or pass holder + 3 adults (children 15 and under free) at per-person sites. One pass covers the whole car at per-vehicle sites (https://fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/legacy-media/r05/America%20the%20Beautiful%20Brochure-English.pdf).
- **Access Pass** may give a 50% discount on some expanded amenity fees (camping, swimming, boat launch, specialized interpretive services); sometimes only for the pass holder. It generally does NOT cover concessioner fees or special recreation permits (https://nps.gov/piro/planyourvisit/americathebeautiful.htm; https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm).
- Access Pass eligibility: any age, medically determined permanent disability that severely limits one or more major life activities (not necessarily 100%). Documentation: photo ID plus ONE of: a licensed physician's signed letter stating (1) the disability is permanent, (2) it severely limits one or more aspects of daily life, (3) the nature of the limitations; OR a federal agency document of disability benefits (VA, SSA); OR a state agency document (e.g., Vocational Rehabilitation). Source: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/accessibility/interagency-access-pass.htm
- Three routes (check the NPS Access Pass page for the current options): (1) in person at 1,000+ federal recreation sites (recommended; physical pass); (2) **digital pass through Recreation.gov** - saved to a mobile device and usable immediately, no wait; (3) by mail from the USGS online store (physical pass only; up to 3 weeks; order receipts are not valid as passes; NPS suggests ordering at least 3 weeks ahead). Online USGS orders are physical passes, not digital ones. Same eligibility documentation applies to all routes. Passes are non-refundable, non-transferable, not replaceable if lost; photo ID required with each use.
- Does NOT cover state parks (they need a state pass; see below). Because the pass is non-transferable, plan for the pass holder to be in the vehicle.
- **NPS fee-free days:** look up the trip year's dates and eligibility on nps.gov (fee-free parks page) and state the date checked. Timed-entry or reservation fees may still apply.
- Timed entry: some popular parks require timed-entry reservations through recreation.gov in peak season; check the park's own page.

## State parks
- Many states sell an annual state parks pass, sometimes bundled with vehicle registration (opt-in or opt-out). Before suggesting any other park pass, check whether the family's registration already included one.
- Some states offer a reduced-price pass for residents with a disability or for income-eligible residents. Eligibility rules can be stricter than the federal Access Pass, and a federal Access Pass is often not accepted as proof. Check your state parks site before suggesting one.
- Daily vehicle passes may cost more at the busiest parks and for out-of-state plates.

## Regional cultural free days
- Some regions fund cultural free days through a local tax district or arts fund. Check the region's calendar and each venue's own page.
- Rules vary by venue: free-day tickets often release online months ahead and sell out, some use lotteries, and some need no ticket. Extras such as theaters or planetariums usually cost more.
- Free days are crowded. For crowd- or noise-sensitive members, compare a paid low-sensory session or a quiet weekday against the free day, and say so.
- Smaller organizations are often free every day.

## Library museum and park passes
- Many public libraries lend free museum or park passes, or state-park backpacks with a hang tag. Check the family's own library.
- Typical rules: a card in good standing, limited uses per month, passes released on a rolling schedule, and party limits (often up to 4), so a larger family may need extra tickets. Park backpacks usually cover entry only, not camping or licenses.

## Income-based discounts (only if eligible)
- **Museums for All:** free or reduced (usually $3 or less) admission at participating museums with a SNAP EBT card (https://museums4all.org). Party limits, time windows and reservation rules vary by venue; some states' human-services agencies also publish an EBT discount list. EBT card is shown, not used to pay; general admission only.
- Confirm eligibility privately with the requester or an authorized contact only if needed. Keep it out of shared documents.

## Off-peak timing
- Cheapest days/times are flexible ones: midweek or Saturday and early-morning flights (DOT Fly-Rights, archived guidance: https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/fly-rights); Google cites Monday-Wednesday departures and booking early for international (https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/how-to-save-money-google-travel-2024/).
- Event tiers: many venues price seasonal events by night; value weeknights can cost noticeably less than weekend nights. Check the venue's own page.

## Fare tracking
- **Google Flights** (https://support.google.com/travel/answer/6235879): turn on "Track prices" for a route (specific dates or "Any dates"), set the passenger count first so prices reflect the whole party; emails on significant changes and "likely to go up" insights. Price graph and date grid show the cheapest dates (https://support.google.com/travel/answer/7664728). "Cheapest" tab may include self-transfer or airport changes; avoid those with mobility devices.
- For long-running alerts, use an available price tracker that suppresses duplicate notifications.

## Booking protections (DOT)
- **24-hour rule:** for tickets bought 7+ days before departure, the airline must either let you cancel within 24 hours for a full refund or hold the fare 24 hours without payment (14 CFR 259.5(b)(4), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-259/section-259.5; https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds). Does not apply to online travel agencies or other third parties. Changes (dates, name fixes) are not free under this rule.
- **Automatic refunds** (DOT final rule announced 2024-04-24, https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/biden-harris-administration-announces-final-rule-requiring-automatic-refunds-airline): refund if the airline cancels or significantly changes a flight (3+ hours domestic, 6+ international; different airport; more connections; downgrade; or connections/aircraft **less accessible** to a person with a disability) and you decline alternatives; checked-bag fee refund if bags are 12+ hours late on domestic flights; refund of unprovided paid extras (seat selection, Wi-Fi). Refunds in original form of payment within 7 business days (card) or 20 days (other).
- **Family seating:** DOT dashboard (https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-family-seating-dashboard) is the starting point. Check current carrier participation, eligible ages, booking conditions and assignment timing. Verify the status of any nationwide requirement against current primary sources.

## Points and rewards (basics only; money decisions go to whoever handles the household's money decisions)
Source: CFPB, https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb%5Fcredit-card-rewards%5Fissue-spotlight%5F2024-05.pdf and https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2024-07-design-marketing-and-administration-of-credit-card-rewards-programs/
- Three types: cash back (simplest, fixed value), co-brand miles (value set by the airline/hotel at redemption), issuer points (often ~1 cent each; value varies by redemption).
- Programs can devalue earned points, impose buried conditions, or lose points in transfer failures; points can vanish when an account closes.
- **Cardholders who carry a balance earned about 27% of rewards but paid 94% of interest and fees** at major issuers (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/more-competition-and-less-complexity-how-the-cfpb-is-working-to-lower-prices-in-the-credit-card-market/). Rule: never suggest a card or points strategy as a saving; at most, note an existing points balance the family already has as a way to cover a fare, and route any card decision to whoever handles the household's money decisions.
