# Accessibility rights and procedures (U.S.)

Checked 2026-11-14. Re-verify before relying on a number for a live trip.

## Air travel: Air Carrier Access Act (ACAA), 14 CFR Part 382
Source: https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/passengers-disabilities

- Applies to all flights of U.S. airlines and to flights to/from the U.S. on foreign airlines. The ADA does **not** cover airplanes; the ACAA does (https://www.ada.gov/topics/service-animals/).
- Airlines may not require advance notice that a person with a disability is traveling. They **may** require up to **48 hours'** notice for accommodations needing preparation (e.g., respirator hook-up, electric wheelchair on aircraft with fewer than 60 seats).
- Airlines may not require a companion except limited safety-assistant cases; if the airline insists, it cannot charge for the assistant.
- No seat may be denied or required because of disability except under FAA exit-row rules.
- Aircraft: new aircraft with 30+ seats need movable aisle armrests on half the aisle seats; new twin-aisle aircraft need accessible lavatories; 100+ seats need in-cabin priority space for a folding wheelchair; aircraft over 60 seats with an accessible lavatory must carry an on-board wheelchair (without one, request it with 48 hours' notice).
- Prompt assistance with boarding, deplaning and connections is required. Ramps or lifts for most aircraft with 19+ seats at U.S. airports with 10,000+ annual enplanements when level boarding is unavailable.
- Assistive devices do not count toward carry-on limits; folding wheelchairs get priority cabin storage if the passenger preboards; wheelchairs get priority in the hold.
- Battery wheelchairs must be accepted (airline provides hazmat packaging free). FAA-labeled portable oxygen concentrators may be used in flight. No charge for required accommodations; optional services (e.g., airline oxygen) may be charged.
- Airlines must have trained Complaints Resolution Officials (CROs) available and must answer written complaints.

### 2024 wheelchair rule (Federal Register 2024-29731, published 2024-12-17)
Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/17/2024-29731/ensuring-safe-accommodations-for-air-travelers-with-disabilities-using-wheelchairs
- Assistance must be **safe and dignified** (14 CFR 382.11(b)); "dignified" includes not delaying restroom access and speaking to the passenger rather than the companion.
- "Mishandled" wheelchair = lost, delayed, damaged or pilfered; mishandling is a per se ACAA violation.
- Airline must tell a passenger before departure whether their checked wheelchair/scooter was loaded.
- If mishandled, airline must notify in writing of the rights to: file a claim, a loaner wheelchair, choose a preferred repair/replacement vendor, and contact a CRO.
- Gap: phase-in dates for individual provisions were not verified. Check the rule before citing a specific deadline to an airline.

## Airport security: TSA
- **TSA Cares / Passenger Support Specialist (PSS):** request at least **72 hours** before departure via the online TSA Cares form (written route): https://www.tsa.gov/contact-center/form/cares. Source: https://www.tsa.gov/travel/passenger-support. PSS does not exempt anyone from screening and is not expedited screening. Qualifying examples: difficulty following instructions due to a disability, traveling with a child with a disability or medical condition, medically necessary liquids over 3.4 oz, service animal.
- **Airport wheelchair service is provided by the airline, not TSA** (same source).
- **Notification card:** tell officers verbally, with the TSA Notification Card (https://tsa.gov/sites/default/files/disability_notification_card_508.pdf), or with medical documentation. Source: https://www.tsa.gov/travel/tsa-cares/disabilities-and-medical-conditions
- Body scanner (AIT) requires standing unassisted with arms raised 5-7 seconds; otherwise pat-down by a same-sex officer (same source). TSA PreCheck passengers unable to stand may remain seated in their mobility device.
- Do not remove attached insulin pumps/monitors; tell the officer where they are. Hearing aids and cochlear implants stay in.
- **Medications and liquids** (https://tsa.gov/travel/travel-tips; https://tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/medical): liquid medication over 3.4 oz is allowed in carry-on in reasonable quantities; declare it at the start of screening; no zip-top bag needed; pills unlimited if screened; TSA does not require prescription bottles (state labeling laws may apply); you may request visual inspection instead of X-ray before items enter the tunnel. Medically required juice/food and formula/breast milk over 3.4 oz are allowed with extra screening.
- Complaints: ask for a supervisor at the checkpoint; the written route is the TSA Contact Center web form https://tsa.gov/contact/contact-forms.

## Airport supports
- **Hidden Disabilities Sunflower lanyard:** a voluntary program in which wearing the lanyard discreetly signals that the wearer may need extra time or help. Participating airports often hand lanyards out free at information desks with no proof required. Ask your departure airport (its accessibility page or written contact form) whether it takes part and where to pick one up.
- **Wings for Autism / Wings for All** (The Arc): free airport rehearsal of check-in, TSA, the boarding area and boarding a plane that does not take off. Source: https://thearc.org/our-initiatives/travel/. Events are hosted by individual airports with local Arc chapters, usually need pre-registration, and may cap the number of support people per participant. Ask your departure airport or local Arc chapter whether it runs rehearsals and when the next one is.

## Lodging: ADA Title III
- **Reservations rule, 28 CFR 36.302(e)** (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-36/subpart-C/section-36.302): hotels must let people reserve accessible rooms during the same hours and in the same ways as others; describe accessible features in enough detail to judge independently; hold accessible rooms until all other rooms of that type are rented; on request, block the specific accessible room and remove it from all reservation systems; and guarantee that specific room. Exception: individually owned units not substantially controlled by the operator (some condo-hotels, timeshares).
- Third-party sites: hotels must make reasonable efforts to give accessible rooms and feature information to some third parties, but are not liable if the third party fails to pass it on (https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations). **Practical rule: book accessible rooms direct.**
- Staff should be able to answer questions about accessible routes, room and bathroom configuration, equipment (bath benches, visual alarms) and common areas (https://adata.org/factsheet/lodging/).
- **Room counts, 2010 ADA Standards Table 224.2** (https://corada.com/documents/2010ADAStandards/224):

| Total guest rooms | Mobility rooms (total) | Of which roll-in shower |
|---|---|---|
| 1-25 | 1 | 0 |
| 26-50 | 2 | 0 |
| 51-75 | 4 | 1 |
| 76-100 | 5 | 1 |
| 101-150 | 7 | 2 |
| 151-200 | 8 | 2 |
| 201-300 | 10 | 3 |
| 301-400 | 12 | 4 |
| 401-500 | 13 | 4 |
| 501-1000 | 3% | 1% |

  **Hotels with 50 or fewer rooms are not required to have any roll-in shower** (https://adata.org/faq-page/hospitality). Communication-feature rooms are a separate count (Table 224.4).
- Roll-in showers in transient lodging must have a folding seat (2010 Standards 608.4) with controls and handheld shower within reach of the seat; the two most common faults are no seat and out-of-reach controls (practitioner summary: https://wheelchairtravel.org/hotels/ada-design-requirements).
- Older buildings may only meet "readily achievable" barrier removal, so "accessible" may not mean full compliance (Title III regulations, above). Ask for photos or measurements in writing.

## Service animals (ADA)
Source: https://www.ada.gov/topics/service-animals/
- A service animal is a dog individually trained to perform a task related to a disability; comfort alone does not qualify (emotional support animals are not service animals under the ADA).
- Allowed most places the public goes, even with no-pets rules. Staff may ask only two questions (is it required because of a disability; what task is it trained to perform). Vests are not required.
- May be excluded if out of control or not housebroken, or where presence fundamentally alters the service (e.g., sterile areas).
- Venue-specific limits exist: zoos may limit service animals in some animal areas, and some adaptive programs (e.g., guided track-chair outings) may not allow them on the trail. Ask the venue in writing before the visit.

## Written complaint routes (when suitable or requested)
- Airline first: email or web form to the airline's consumer office; airlines must acknowledge within 30 days and respond in writing within 60 days. Then the DOT online form https://airconsumer.dot.gov/consumer/s/oacp-form (disability complaints are forwarded to the airline, which must respond to you and DOT). Source: https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/file-consumer-complaint
- TSA: web form above.
- ADA (hotels, venues, state and local government): https://www.ada.gov/file-a-complaint/ routes air travel to DOT, housing to HUD, employment to EEOC, everything else to the DOJ Civil Rights Division online report at https://civilrights.justice.gov/ (mail is the other option). DOJ review can take up to three months.
- Phone-only note: DOT also runs a disability hotline (800-778-4838 voice, per CDC https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/disability). It is an option, never the required action.
