# Accessible Family Trips

Expert method for low-cost outings and occasional larger trips for families, including members with significant disability, medical or sensory needs. Accessibility and budget are **gates**, not afterthoughts: an option that fails either never reaches the decision-maker.

## When to Use
- Any request for a family outing, day trip, vacation, fare/lodging deal, or travel logistics.
- Any plan involving a member with mobility, sensory, medical or wandering needs.
- Not for: buying or booking (never), medical decisions (clinician), money decisions (whoever handles the household's money decisions).

Use only trip-relevant details the requester supplies or authorizes you to access. Keep personal, health, budget and location details out of public artifacts.

References (legal and program examples below are U.S.-specific; verify applicability to the itinerary and use local equivalents elsewhere):
- `references/accessibility-rights.md` - ADA, Air Carrier Access Act, TSA, lodging, service animals, written complaint routes
- `references/budget-methods.md` - passes, free days, library and income-based programs, fares, 24-hour rule, refunds, family seating, points
- `references/checklists-contingency.md` - pre-trip checklist, medical go-bag, wandering plan, altitude, sensory plan
- `references/sources.md` - every source, with conflicts noted

Use an available fare tracker, route planner or citation workflow where useful.

## Method

### 1. Build the party profile (before any search)
From authorized, task-relevant inputs, record only what this trip requires:
- Party size and ages (ticket tiers change at ages like 2, 3, 12, 13, 16, 18; see references).
- For each member with needs: mobility (walking distance, device, transfers), sensory (noise, crowds, light), elopement/wandering risk, medical (meds, devices, refrigeration, seizure or breathing plans), toileting/changing needs, stamina window.
- Verify fixed commitments and any dates or time windows the party wants to avoid.
- Budget ceiling and who approves spending.
- Channel rules: Use a supported channel the requester can use and prefers; if a process truly needs an excluded channel, say so plainly.
If a needs fact is missing, design for the most demanding plausible case and list the assumption in your output. Never guess a diagnosis.

### 2. Generate candidates cheapest-first
Work down this ladder and stop when you have 4-6 candidates:
1. Always-free public land and venues (city/county open space, wildlife refuges, free parks).
2. Free via a pass the family holds or can get free: federal Access Pass, state parks pass (some states sell it with vehicle registration), library backpack passes, free-ticket seasons.
3. Free days (regional cultural free days; NPS fee-free days) - but weigh crowds against sensory needs.
4. Income-based discounts (Museums for All / EBT) only if the family qualifies; never assume.
5. Paid, with the cheapest date/tier (value nights, off-peak, member rates) and the free alternative beside it.

### 3. Accessibility gate (every candidate)
Pass only if you can cite, from the venue's own page or a primary source dated this season:
- Accessible parking AND an accessible route from parking to the main experience (approach barriers count: a 12% bridge before an "accessible" trail fails a manual chair).
- Trail/route facts: surface, width, grade, length. Prefer paved/concrete; treat "usable" or "wheelchair friendly" as weaker than "ADA accessible".
- Accessible restroom open at the time of visit (seasonal closures are common); note adult changing tables where needed.
- Sensory: quiet space, sensory kits, noise level, crowd pattern, an easy early exit. Prefer sensory-friendly sessions and Certified Autism Center/KultureCity venues when sensory needs exist.
- Elopement: water, roads, open edges near the route; a plan for supervision handoffs.
- Altitude, temperature and weather for the date (sleeping above ~8,000 ft carries altitude-illness risk; day trips are much less stressful).
- Loaner equipment (wheelchairs, track chairs) and whether it can be reserved in writing.
An option missing any required access fact does NOT pass the gate and cannot be a ranked recommendation. It may appear only as a clearly labeled conditional, non-recommended lead pending verification, with the missing fact stated next to it and a supported verification route the requester can use; never omitted. Gaps in non-required facts (e.g., minor amenities) are stated beside a passing option.

### 4. Budget gate (every candidate)
- Price the **whole party** all-in: tickets by age tier, fees, parking, fuel, food plan, equipment rental. Base fares and per-person prices are not the cost.
- Compare to the ceiling; show the cheaper alternative for anything with a price.
- Check refundability and change rules before recommending anything prepaid; prefer free cancellation.
- Never buy, book, reserve or submit. Prepare the cart, link or form and hand it over. Free reservations that need the family's account are also handed over, not made.

### 5. Larger trips: extra steps
- Fares: track with Google Flights price tracking ("Any dates" if flexible) or set a watch with another available fare tracker; quote all-in price for the full party, including bags and seats.
- Seat the family together: prefer airlines on DOT's fee-free family-seating dashboard; keep everyone on one reservation; choose or skip seats for the whole booking.
- Book 7+ days out so the DOT 24-hour cancel/hold rule applies; book direct with the airline (the rule does not cover third-party agents).
- Disability logistics: Choose a verified pass route that will be usable in time (see `references/budget-methods.md`). Request airline assistance in writing at booking. Verify the airport's screening-assistance process, notice window and late-request route (see `references/accessibility-rights.md` for a U.S. example). Ask the airport about accessible orientation and rehearsal options. Date the checks.
- Lodging: verify the specific accessible room type (roll-in vs. transfer shower vs. tub, communication features) on the property's own site, then ask the property to confirm in writing that the specific room is blocked for you. Book direct.
- Medical: health-care letter, meds for the trip plus extra in carry-on, destination pharmacy/urgent care/ER located. Clinician review 4-6 weeks out for complex conditions.

### 6. Present the decision
- Two or three options, already ranked, best first. One line why it is first.
- For each: date(s), all-in cost, what was checked (access, sensory, budget), gaps, and the **next real task** ("Reserve the free 10 a.m. ticket at this link").
- One fallback for bad weather or a hard day.
- Hand useful calendar entries to the requester or an authorized calendar steward.
- Save plans only in an approved destination if requested; private details stay out of shared documents.

## Stop and refer
- **Emergency:** contact local emergency services. If a person at risk of wandering is missing, report nearby water and roads and follow responders’ directions.
- **Medical fitness to travel, altitude risk for someone with heart/lung/neurologic conditions, medication timing across time zones, oxygen or devices in flight:** the member's licensed clinician decides; you prepare the questions.
- **Altitude illness symptoms that worsen, confusion, or breathlessness at rest:** descend and seek emergency care; do not plan around it.
- **Disability-rights disputes:** use the written routes in `accessibility-rights.md` (airline complaint, DOT online form, TSA contact form, DOJ ADA report). For anything needing legal judgment, a lawyer or disability-rights organization decides.
- **Money questions beyond a quote** (can we afford it, which card): whoever handles the household's money decisions.

## Common mistakes
- Quoting per-person or base prices instead of the full party's all-in cost.
- Trusting a single "accessible" label without checking the approach route, restroom season, and grade.
- Planning a free day for a crowd-sensitive member without flagging that it can be crowded.
- Assuming an Access Pass covers state parks or concessions (it covers federal entrance/standard amenity fees only).
- Splitting a family across reservations, losing family-seating protection.
- Booking via an online travel agent and losing the DOT 24-hour rule.
- Offering an unusable contact channel without checking for accessible alternatives.
- Long itineraries with no downtime; sightseeing on travel days.
- Using stale prices or dates: always re-check the venue page for this season and say the check date.
- Proposing more than three options or an unranked list.
