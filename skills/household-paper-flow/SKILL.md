# Household paper flow and records

## Purpose
Give a household one paper flow that empties weekly, a clear retention rule for every document
type, safe storage for vital documents, a scanning and shredding routine, and an
in-case-of-emergency binder. General information, not tax or legal advice; for specifics ask a
CPA, attorney or the agency. US-centric (IRS, FTC, CFPB); verify rules for other countries.

## When to use
- Paper piles, an unopened-mail backlog, or "can I toss this?" questions.
- Setting up filing (paper or digital), scanning, shredding, or a tax-year archive.
- Protecting vital documents, child identity, photos and memorabilia.
- Building or updating an emergency binder.

## Method

Researched 2026-11-09. General information only, not tax or legal advice; for specifics ask a CPA, attorney or the agency. Tags: [IRS] [FTC] [CFPB] [FDA] [LOC] [NARA] [ready.gov] = from that opened source (see Sources). "(judgment)" = practitioner rule.

## 0. The 30-line version

**Flow:** Mail/paper arrives -> ONE inbox tray -> process weekly (15 min) into five outcomes: **Act** (due date on a calendar, then file), **File** (reference), **Scan** (then decide if the paper stays), **Shred** (anything with account numbers, SSNs, medical or child data), **Recycle**.

**Keep, by default [FTC 2025; IRS]:**

| Keep | Items |
|---|---|
| ~1 year | Bank statements, pay stubs, credit card and utility bills, undisputed medical bills, deposited checks (then shred) |
| 3 years minimum | Income tax returns and the records behind them (W-2s, 1099s, receipts); home-sale records |
| 6 / 7 years | Longer IRS windows: 6 years if you omitted more than 25 percent of gross income; 7 years for worthless-securities or bad-debt claims [IRS] |
| While you own it, plus the tax window after sale | Vehicle title, deed, mortgage and loan papers, home improvement receipts, major-appliance receipts and warranties, leases |
| Forever (locked) | Birth, adoption, marriage, divorce, death certificates; Social Security cards; passports and citizenship papers; wills, living wills, powers of attorney; retirement and pension records; military records; vital health records |

**Shred [FTC]:** ATM receipts, credit/insurance offers, cleared checks after 14 days, credit reports, old prescription information, expired warranties, expired cards and IDs. If you can reach a document electronically (bank portal), you can shred the paper statement.

**Conservative note:** FTC's 2015 shredding post said keep tax returns forever and other tax records 7 years; its 2025 list says at least 3 years. IRS gives the legal windows (3 / 6 / 7 years / indefinitely). Recommended house policy (judgment): keep tax returns permanently (they are small, and scanned), keep supporting records 7 years.

**Three copies of vital documents:** paper original locked and fireproof/waterproof; encrypted scan; and one copy off-site (safe-deposit box, trusted person, or encrypted cloud). [ready.gov]

**Before renovation or construction:** move vital-records storage (document safe, fire box, emergency binder) out of the work area or off-site before work starts; dust, water shut-offs, open walls and contractors coming and going all put it at risk. Update the binder's home inventory when the work is done. (judgment)

## 1. Set up the paper flow (60-90 minutes, once)

1. **Stage one inbox** by the front door or office: tray plus a recycling bin and a shred box beside it. Opening mail over the shred bin kills junk instantly.
2. **Stop it at the source (30 min):** switch banks, utilities, insurers and medical portals to e-statements; opt out of prescreened credit offers (optoutprescreen.com is the official site; not opened here, verify); unsubscribe from catalogs.
3. **Action file:** a 12-slot monthly folder or a single "Act" folder with sticky dates, plus calendar entries for due dates. Bills go to autopay when possible.
4. **Reference cabinet:** one drawer, 10-12 labeled hanging files (below). Adding a file requires removing one.
5. **Weekly 15-minute process:** same day each week; the inbox is empty at the end.
6. **Quarterly 30-minute purge** against the retention table: shred statements older than a year, tax-year file closes after 7 years (annual routine in `references/retention-details.md`).
7. **Annual January session (1-2 h):** open the new tax-year folder; move last year's supporting records into the archive box labeled "destroy after [year]."

**What goes wrong:** the "to be filed" pile that survives months; keeping every statement "just in case"; a single scanning marathon that is never repeated; paper from kids' schools mixed with household bills.

**Recommended top-level folders (judgment):** Taxes (by year) / Banking and Debts / Insurance / Home and Vehicles / Medical / Kids (one folder per child) / Employment and Benefits / Legal and Estate / Warranties and Manuals / Manuals can be a digital-only folder.

## 4. Vital documents

**Locked originals (fire/water-rated box or safe-deposit box; keep the key off-site):** birth certificates, Social Security cards, passports, marriage/divorce/death certificates, wills and directives, titles and deeds, military records, adoption papers. [FTC; ready.gov]

**Never carry** the Social Security card; carry a copy only if needed.

**Fireproof boxes (judgment):** look for a stated fire rating and a water rating from a testing lab on the box; they protect paper, not USB drives or discs unless rated.

**Replacement info:** note on the binder cover who issues each (county clerk for vital records, Social Security, State Department). Time to replace can be weeks; keep a scan.


## 7. Shredding

- Shred anything with account or SSN numbers, signatures, medical or child data, and pre-approved offers. [FTC]
- No shredder: community shred days (ask your credit union or city; no local dates verified), a shredding service, or at minimum block out account numbers with a marker. [FTC]
- Prescription bottles: blacken labels with a marker. [FTC]
- Shredded paper: many recycling programs want it at a drop-off location, not in curbside bins. Confirm with the local hauler or recycling center.
- Time: a cross-cut personal shredder handles about 8-10 sheets; do small batches; do not shred paper clips or staples if the machine says no. (judgment)
- Keep a shred bin, empty monthly.

## Scanning, photos and memorabilia
Scan only what has a reason (warranties, receipts you may need, insurance, medical, vital
documents, kids' records); 200-300 dpi grayscale is enough for documents; name files
`YYYY-MM-DD_Source_What.pdf`; back up 3-2-1 and encrypt. For photos sort first, scan second, keep a
TIFF master. Full workflow, resolutions and archival storage: `references/scanning-photos-memorabilia.md`.

## Emergency binder
A grab-and-go folder plus encrypted scan with contacts and plan, ID copies, financial and legal
summary, medical summary and home inventory; updated each January and after major events.
Contents and storage: `references/emergency-binder.md`.

## 10. Quick decision cards for the agent

- "Can I toss this bank statement?" -> If the statement is older than 1 year and not needed for taxes, a claim or a loan, shred it. If it supports a deduction, keep it with that year's tax records.
- "Receipts for a TV?" -> Keep while you own it and for the warranty, then shred.
- "Old tax returns?" -> Keep the return PDF; supporting records 7 years.
- "House sold years ago?" -> Keep sale records for the property's tax window, and improvement receipts that affect basis; ask a CPA.
- "Report cards?" -> Scan, keep the originals in the kid's binder.
- "Medical bills?" -> About one year unless a dispute is open; keep EOBs for large claims and anything tied to a tax deduction.

## Pitfalls
- A "to be filed" pile that survives months; one scanning marathon that is never repeated.
- Keeping every statement "just in case" when the bank portal already holds it.
- Passwords written in the binder (store the password-manager recovery key in the safe instead).
- Only paper copies, kept in a damp basement; binders made once and never updated.
- Quoting tax thresholds from memory; they change yearly.

## Verification
- The inbox tray is empty at the end of each weekly session.
- Every kept document maps to a row of the retention table; vital originals are locked with a scan
  and an off-site copy.
- The emergency binder has an update date within the last year.
