# Evening check-in, reflection and goals

## Purpose
Close the day for a person in a few calm minutes: what tomorrow needs, any decision planning could
not make, one reflection question, and a parked-thoughts capture so they can sleep. Once a week the
check-in becomes a short review of hour targets and goals. Goals are set and reviewed with
psychology-based methods and feed the planner as targets, not as private detail.

## When to use
- A scheduled evening nudge, or the person says "check in", "let's reflect", "help me set a goal".
- The weekly review slot the household chose.
- Not a planning session: if tomorrow needs rebuilding, hand it to the planner and keep the
  check-in short.

## Method

### 1. Tone and shape
The person is winding down, often in bed with the phone about to go on the charger. Short, calm,
no lecture. On chat apps use short separate messages (at most about 4). If they do not answer, do
not chase; no reply tonight is fine.

### 2. Order
1. **Tomorrow** (3-5 lines), from the planner's "tomorrow" summary or the calendar: first event,
   leave-by times, mandatory items, anything to bring or prepare tonight.
2. **Decisions:** anything planning could not decide, one line each, with your recommendation.
3. **One reflection question**, rotated; never more than one unless they want to keep going:
   - "What went well today, and what made it work?"
   - "What got in the way? Anything to change tomorrow?"
   - "On a 1-5, how was today's energy?"
   - "Anything on your mind you want parked so you can sleep?" Capture it as a task, then say it
     is captured.
4. Done.

**Verify before sending.** Read tomorrow's actual calendar again just before the message goes out,
even if a planner summary exists. Drop anything that is not this person's own obligation (another
household member's work reminder or note), duplicated blocks, and "decisions" that are really
settled recurring facts (who does which drop-off, where an appointment happens). Keep those settled
facts in the person's local skill and check each decision line against them. A wrong or stale line
costs more trust than a missing one: list only what is real.

### 3. Weekly review (replaces step 3 once a week)
- Hours this week per category vs the person's targets.
- Ask: keep, raise or lower each target next week? Write the answer where the planner reads targets.
- Goal check: one line per active goal: progress, keep / adjust / drop.

### 4. Setting and reviewing goals
- **Values first** (ACT): ask what matters and why before the what; tie each goal to a value the
  person names.
- **Few:** at most 3 active goals. Approach goals ("do X"), not avoidance goals ("stop Y").
- **Specific and measurable** (goal-setting theory, Locke & Latham): concrete, hard but reachable,
  with a weekly measure the planner can box (hours or sessions).
- **WOOP / mental contrasting** (Oettingen): Wish, Outcome, main inner Obstacle, Plan as an
  implementation intention "If <obstacle>, then I <action>" (Gollwitzer). Record the if-then plan
  with the goal.
- **Process over outcome:** track the habit or hours, not only the result; celebrate consistency,
  not perfection.
- **Self-compassion** (Neff): after a miss, normalize it and re-plan. Missed is not failed.
- **ADHD-aware:** external, visible tracking (the planner boxes it); short feedback loops
  (weekly); novelty is fuel, so let the person refresh how a goal is pursued without dropping it.
- **Feed planning:** each active goal becomes an hour target or recurring box in the planner's
  data doc, as a short name and target only. Keep the goal's reasons and private detail in the
  assistant's private notes, never in shared files.

### 5. Logging
After each check-in append one line to a private log: date, energy (if given), win, obstacle,
parked items. Daily planning reads the last ~7 lines to adjust load (a low-energy streak means a
lighter day).

## Pitfalls
- More than one reflection question, or advice nobody asked for.
- Chasing an unanswered check-in.
- Writing private goal reasons or reflections into shared planning files.
- Re-planning tomorrow inside the check-in instead of routing it to the planner.
- Shame wording about missed targets.
- Sending the planner's summary unchecked: someone else's reminders, duplicate blocks, or a
  long-settled recurring arrangement presented as an open decision.

## Verification
- Tomorrow's first event, leave-by times and mandatory items were stated from real calendar data,
  re-read just before sending; every listed item and decision is the person's own and still open.
- Any parked thought became a task, and the person was told it was captured.
- The log line was appended; target changes from the weekly review were written where the planner
  reads them.

## Household specifics
The agent's own local skill supplies the check-in time and channel, where the "tomorrow" summary,
goals file and check-in log live, which day the weekly review runs, the person's category names
and hour targets, and any message-rhythm rules for that person.
