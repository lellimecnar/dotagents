# Chapter openings and endings

## Openings
- **Surface problem tied to the story-worthy problem** (Les Edgerton, Hooked, 2007). The inciting incident
  creates an initial surface problem and gives the first inkling of the deeper story-worthy problem
  (Writer's Digest excerpt "Write Fiction that Grabs Readers from Page One"). At chapter level: the
  opening problem should be concrete and touch the book's spine (the protagonist's core wound or want).
- **Stealth thesis paragraph** (Sanderson, 2025 Lecture 2): the best chapters open with a paragraph that
  quietly tells the reader what the chapter is about, and close with a "stinger" line.
- **Tone promise** (Sanderson, 2020 Lecture 2): the opening teaches the reader what kind of chapter this is.
- Avoid: **White Room** openings (a character waking in a featureless set and pondering), **Frontloading**
  and **Nowhere Nowhen** (Turkey City Lexicon).
- A continuation hook after a cliffhanger is fine. Open on the reaction (a sequel), not on a recap.

## Endings
- End on something "which makes your reader genuinely curious or in crisis" (Sanderson, BYU 2020 Lecture 4
  Q&A, notes by G. Skaggs). The cut-mid-sentence "She opened the door and—" is a "dirty trick" that still
  works. A real turn is better.
- Swain's scenes end in disaster, so a chapter that ends on a scene's disaster gets the next chapter's
  opening reaction for free.
- Time jumps: a chapter or part break carries "three weeks later" more easily than a mid-chapter summary
  (Sanderson, 2025 Lecture 2).
- No processing paragraph after a peak; use action as transition. Terminal beats are short and concrete.

## Tests
1. In the first 150 words, can you name the chapter's surface problem? Is any recap ≤ 1 clause?
2. Does the first paragraph work as a stealth thesis?
3. Is the last line a disaster or a question, and is it concrete?
4. Is there a sentence after the peak that explains the peak? Cut it.
5. Mirror lines deliberately echoed in another chapter keep their wording.
6. Before cutting or rewriting an opening or ending, check prior assessments of the chapter for passages
   marked to preserve, and note where your change agrees or differs.
